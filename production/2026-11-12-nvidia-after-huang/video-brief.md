### Read-through (cues removed, for approval)

**Hook**
Jensen Huang just left Nvidia. And his stake was only three point five eight percent. Here is what the filings say about the board, the ownership, and the supply chain.

**What happened**
On November eleventh, Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as chief executive and director, effective immediately. The filing gives no reason. So we stick to what the documents actually say. CFO Colette Kress, fifty-nine, becomes interim CEO, and keeps her finance role while the search runs. Kress has been CFO since twenty thirteen, after finance roles at Cisco, Microsoft and Texas Instruments. Harvey Jones, a director since nineteen ninety-three, becomes the board's first-ever chair. The board will weigh internal and external candidates, and earnings stay on schedule for November seventeenth.

**Ownership**
The proxy puts his stake at eight hundred seventy million shares, as of March twenty-third. Fifty-nine million of those shares sit in a family foundation he has no financial interest in. Leave those out, and his latest filing adds up to about three point three percent. At Wednesday's closing price, that stake was worth about one hundred forty-one billion dollars. Nvidia has one class of stock, so Huang had no special voting power. Vanguard alone holds seven point three percent, more than twice his stake.

**The business**
The business he leaves is still growing. Last quarter's revenue was ninety-six point two billion dollars. Guidance for this quarter is one hundred eight billion dollars, plus or minus two percent. Two customers alone made up thirty-six percent of last fiscal year's sales.

**Supply chain**
Nvidia designs chips but does not make them. TSMC and Samsung make the wafers. Memory comes from SK Hynix, Micron and Samsung, in a supply chain concentrated mainly in Asia. To lock in that supply, Nvidia has paid premiums, made deposits, and signed long-term capacity agreements. Its supply and purchase obligations total ninety-five point two billion dollars. TSMC told investors in October that demand is still far above what it can supply. So who negotiates those supply allocations day to day? The public record does not say. What it does show is Huang as the public face of Nvidia's biggest government deals. In Saudi Arabia, a first phase of eighteen thousand chips, and several hundred thousand over five years. In Washington, one analyst called him Nvidia's lead voice on China. Yet China is now effectively closed to its data center chips, according to Nvidia's own annual report.

**What it means**
Nvidia's own risk factor points to its executive team, not to one person. As of Thursday, we found no supplier statement on the change. That is the gap to watch.

**What to watch**
Watch November seventeenth: the first earnings call with Kress as interim chief executive. Then the CEO search, any supplier or customer comments, and Congress's request for a briefing.

**Close**
That is the picture from the filings. Full breakdown at the link in bio.
