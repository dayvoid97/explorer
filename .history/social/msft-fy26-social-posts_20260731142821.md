### BODY

MSFT is up ~25% in two sessions and every take I've read is recycling the press release. I pulled the actual FY26 10-K (year ended June 30, 2026) and went through the income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement, and the new tax disclosure. Sharing the parts that I think actually matter.

**The headline numbers:**

- Revenue: $331.8B, up 17.8% (growth *accelerated* at a $330B base)
- Operating income: $155.2B, up 20.8%
- Net income: $133.7B, up 31.3% — a **40.3% net margin**
- Diluted EPS: $17.95 vs $13.64
- Operating cash flow: $182.9B, up 34.4%

**Three things I think most coverage is missing:**

**1. The capex number is historically unprecedented.**
Additions to property and equipment: $44.5B (FY24) → $64.6B (FY25) → **$115.9B (FY26)**. That's +161% in two years. Net PP&E is now $313.1B, up 53% in a single year. A software company now carries the fixed asset base of a Class I railroad.

But here's the part that surprised me: **free cash flow is still ~$67B** ($182.9B OCF − $115.9B capex). They're funding the entire buildout from operations. Total debt is ~$40B against $76.8B in cash and short-term investments — still net cash positive by ~$36B *mid-buildout*. No leverage story here, which is the main thing separating this from 1999.

**2. The revenue mix flipped and nobody's talking about it.**
Service and other revenue: $267.1B, +22.7%, now **over 80% of total revenue**. Product revenue: $64.7B, roughly flat. The box-of-Windows business is a rounding error inside a metered-consumption business now.

The cost of that shift shows up too — service COGS jumped 26.8% to $94.3B, compressing gross margin ~90bps to 67.9%. Feeding GPUs isn't free.

**3. $73B of next year's revenue is already in the bank.**
Short-term unearned revenue rose 13% to **$73.0B**. That's cash customers have already paid for services not yet delivered. Combined with guidance of ~45% Azure growth in constant currency and demand still exceeding available capacity, a large chunk of FY27 is contracted before the year starts.

**Also worth flagging — the accounting change:**
Effective FY27, Microsoft extended the estimated useful lives of its datacenters to 15–25 years (from 6–15). Minimal effect on FY27 operating income, but it shifts future datacenter leases from finance to operating leases and smooths depreciation on a $313B asset base. D&A already jumped 31% to $38.5B. I'd watch this line closely — it embeds an assumption that GPU-dense datacenters stay economically useful for up to 25 years, and hardware history argues for humility there.

**The bear case, from the same filing:**

- $313B of PP&E depreciates whether demand shows up or not. If AI compute demand ever plateaus, those charges run against flat revenue.
- The constraint is now power and grid interconnects, not chips or customers — and MSFT doesn't fully control those timelines.
- After a 25% two-day move, the market has pre-paid for the ~45% Azure guide. Guidance is now the floor, not the target.
- Gross margin is bending, slowly. 90bps is fine; the question is whether AI revenue keeps scaling faster than the cost of serving it.

Happy to go deeper on any of these in the comments — especially the depreciation math, which I think is the most underrated risk in the whole filing.

### COMMENT REPLIES TO HAVE READY

**If someone asks "isn't this just an AI bubble?"**
> Fair question, and the depreciation schedule is exactly where that argument has teeth. The difference vs 1999 is the funding source — this is being paid for out of $183B of operating cash flow with net cash on the balance sheet, not debt or equity issuance. That doesn't mean the returns will be there. It means a demand slowdown produces a margin problem, not a solvency problem.

**If someone says "you're just pumping MSFT":**
> There's a bear section in the post and in the article — depreciation wave, power constraints, margin compression, and the fact that the market has already pre-paid for the Azure guide. I don't hold a position I'm trying to talk up here; I'm mostly interested in the capex-to-FCF math because it's without precedent.

**If someone asks about the Anthropic/OpenAI gains:**
> Other income swung from −$4.9B (FY25) to +$10.7B (FY26), with $11.0B of net recognized gains on investments and derivatives in the cash flow statement. Equity and other investments on the balance sheet more than doubled, $15.4B → $36.3B. Worth separating that from operating performance — operating income grew 20.8% on its own, so the core business isn't leaning on the investment gains.
