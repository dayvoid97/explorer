Monday's rally was historic. Tuesday morning is the bill for it. Nvidia shares were down about 5% in premarket trading, index futures pointed to a lower open across the board, and Anthropic broke below its $83 IPO price for the first time — five days after the largest debut on record. The one place still celebrating was Asia, which had closed before Monday's announcement and spent Tuesday catching up in a single session. All figures below are as of about 7:40 a.m. ET and will move at the open.

## The futures board

Contracts tied to the S&P 500 were down about 1.0% in premarket trading, Nasdaq-100 futures were down about 1.9%, and Dow futures were down about 0.5%, according to Barron's — with MarketWatch putting the S&P 500 contract around session lows. Reuters' earlier 5:26 a.m. snapshot had been milder (S&P 500 down 0.46%, Nasdaq-100 down 1.11%), which tells the direction of the morning: the fade deepened as the open approached. Traders were locking in quick profits on megacap tech and AI names after Monday's surge, Barron's reported, while the Wall Street Journal pointed to lingering questions around the AI trade itself.

## Nvidia gives some back

Nvidia was last down 5.2% at $156.40 in premarket trading, Reuters reported, after Monday's 31.5% surge added more than $952 billion in market value — the largest one-day gain on record. The pullback was broad across chips: Intel and Micron were down about 7%, AMD about 5% and SanDisk about 5.5%, per the Journal, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) off more than 4%. Reuters' read of the morning was that investors were refocusing on the political and governance risks surrounding Jensen Huang's reinstatement — the investigations the board itself said will proceed to conclusion — rather than on the return itself.

## Anthropic breaks its IPO price

Anthropic's ANT shares were last down 6.5% at $82 in premarket trading, Reuters reported — below the $83 IPO price for the first time. The reversal is five days old: ANT priced at $83 on November 18, opened at $111.50 on November 19 with a fully diluted valuation that briefly topped $2 trillion, and has fallen every session since, closing Monday at $87.64 while the rest of the AI trade was rallying. One support sits ahead on the calendar rather than in the price: MSCI plans to add Anthropic to its global standard indexes at the November 30 close, and index-tracking funds will have to buy the stock to match — demand that arrives whether or not the price cooperates.

## Asia's verdict: a surge

Asia closed Monday before the reinstatement was announced, and Tuesday was its first chance to vote. It voted yes, loudly. South Korea's Kospi rose 6.3% to 5,006.03, with SK Hynix up 8.6% and Samsung Electronics up 8.2%; Taiwan's Taiex jumped about 9%, its largest one-day gain in more than a year, with TSMC and Foxconn up nearly 10% each; Japan's Nikkei, reopening after a holiday, rose 3.9% to 58,644.63, with Advantest up 12.5%. MSCI's broadest Asia-Pacific index outside Japan climbed 4.6%. The exception was Hong Kong, where the Hang Seng shed early gains to fall 0.8% to 22,205.65, its lowest in more than a year. Early European trading was quiet by comparison, with the DAX and FTSE 100 each down about 0.1%.

## Rates, oil, gold

The bond market is not pricing relief. The 10-year Treasury yield was around 4.9% early Tuesday — about 4.94% at Monday's close, per Reuters, dipping to about 4.89% in the morning — and the 2-year note yielded about 4.69%. Fed funds futures traders are pricing a rate hike at the December 8–9 FOMC meeting as a certainty, according to CME FedWatch data reported by Reuters, which makes Wednesday's core PCE inflation print — the last major release before the meeting, and before Thursday's Thanksgiving closure — the week's decisive number. Consumer confidence follows at 10 a.m. ET today.

In commodities, oil was steady: WTI crude hovered around $80 a barrel and Brent was roughly flat near $89.50, both little changed after Monday's slide of more than 3%. Gold went the other way from Monday's rebound, down about 0.7% to around $3,624 an ounce, per Investopedia. Bitcoin held just above $60,000, and the dollar index sat near 104.3, close to a 19-month high.

## What to watch at the open

Whether Nvidia's fade stops at profit-taking or turns into a second verdict on the reinstatement; whether ANT can reclaim $83 with the MSCI buying still six days away; and whether the broader market's losses stay in the AI complex or spread the way they did last week. Monday recovered just over half of last week's fall in one session. Tuesday morning is testing how much of that recovery the market actually believes.
