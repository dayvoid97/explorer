On its own, the Sparta II is a one-day story: a sanctioned Russian cargo ship, two drones, a fire, a rescue. Read against the last ten days, it is something else — the third sea in ten days where drones found a ship, and part of a declared campaign against the logistics network Russia now runs out of the Baltic. That is the story worth printing.

## Two Drones Set the Sparta II Ablaze West of Gogland

Two drones hit the Sparta II on Saturday, November 21, in international waters west of Gogland Island in the Gulf of Finland, according to Reuters, reporting Finnish and Russian officials. The 121-metre Russian-flagged ro-ro vessel caught fire, lost steering and drifted into Finnish territorial waters, where Finland led the rescue. All 16 crew evacuated to lifeboats and then to a nearby Russian ship; no casualties were reported. The ship had left St. Petersburg on Friday bound for Baltiysk, the naval port of the Kaliningrad exclave, and Russian vessels later towed it away.

The Sparta II is not a random merchantman. It is owned by Oboronlogistika, the Russian Defence Ministry's logistics company, and is under US sanctions, according to the US Treasury Department. Finland's Border Guard said military materiel aboard could not be ruled out. Leningrad region's governor said 80 drones were shot down over the region the same night, and Finland's military had already detected a strike on a Russian warship in the same area the previous week — the missile corvette Naro-Fominsk, which Ukraine's SBU says it hit during the attack on the Ust-Luga oil terminal.

## Zelenskyy Claims the Strike — and Declares Every Russian-Flagged Cargo Fair Game

On Monday, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed to Bloomberg that Ukraine carried out the strike. He said the Sparta II was a legitimate target because it was carrying weapons — a claim Bloomberg said it could not independently verify — and he stated the rule behind it in terms that go well beyond one ship: "Every vessel with a Russian flag, everything that carries weapons or any sanctioned cargo... is a legitimate target for us in Russian territorial or neutral waters."

Zelenskyy also acknowledged the strike "might have rattled some of his allies" and that some European partners are worried about fighting in the Baltic. The Kremlin said the strike raised tensions in the Baltic Sea, which carries about 15% of the world's freight shipping a year.

## Three Seas in Ten Days

The Sparta II was the middle act of a ten-day sequence, mapped in a November 24 analysis by the European Centre for Strategic Analysis (CEAS):

### Black Sea: Civilian Ships Are Sinking in NATO Waters

- **November 16–17:** the bulk carrier Betul 8, carrying nearly 3,000 tonnes of wheat from Tulcea, Romania, to Egypt, capsized in Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone after its crew reported two sea-drone hits; the next day the Liberian-flagged gas carrier Gas Husky, carrying 3,400 tonnes of propane-butane to Romania, went down off Byala. All crew were rescued. Per the French outlet Mer et Marine, these were the 14th and 15th ships hit since October 5 in Romanian and Bulgarian waters, and the fifth and sixth to sink. The Institute for the Study of War assesses the Betul 8 was likely struck by Russian sea drones; Bulgaria has not formally attributed the attacks.
### Baltic: the Sparta II

- **November 21:** the Sparta II, above.
### Mediterranean: a Corvette Sunk at Benghazi

- **November 22:** Ukraine's security service said seven Ukrainian-made sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi's naval base in Libya, and the Oboronlogistika freighter Angara was damaged alongside it.

Two campaigns sit inside one map. Civilian trade — wheat for Egypt, gas for an EU member — is being sunk in NATO members' economic zones, with attribution pointing at Russia. Russian military logistics is being hunted in three seas at once, with Ukraine claiming the strikes openly.

## The Real Target: the Baltic Sealift That Feeds Libya and the Sahel

Maritime intelligence firm Windward, in a November 24 analysis, ties the Baltic and Benghazi strikes into one target system. The Sparta II and the Angara were part of a coordinated Oboronlogistika fleet that ships weapons and military equipment to Africa. Russia moved this sealift north after Turkey closed the Bosphorus to its warships in February 2022 and after it lost access to Syria: it now supplies its foothold in eastern Libya, and through it the Sahel, from the Baltic. The convoy that sailed in October went down the English Channel, switched off its AIS transponders within days of each other near Gibraltar, and ran dark into the Mediterranean — and was found anyway. Windward notes the same network's ships have previously been linked to military cargo movements involving China, Iran and North Korea, and that several related vessels remain active on the St. Petersburg–Kaliningrad run.

Ukraine has been working this target set for a year. Windward links Kyiv to strikes on the shadow-fleet tanker Qendil in December 2025, the Russian LNG carrier Arctic Metagaz in March, and the ro-ro Lady Mariia in September. After the Arctic Metagaz attack, China-bound Russian LNG carriers stopped transiting the Mediterranean and rerouted around the Cape of Good Hope — a measurable freight-cost consequence of drone range.

The Kaliningrad leg matters most. The exclave sits between Poland and Lithuania, and the St. Petersburg–Baltiysk shuttle the Sparta II had run for the past 12 months is its sea bridge. Kaliningrad's governor publicly insisted the strike posed no threat to supplies to the region — a reassurance that itself marks where the sensitivity lies.

## The Wreck Drifted Into NATO Waters

The burning wreck drifted into the waters of a NATO member, and NATO's newest members ran the response. Finland imposed temporary aviation and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf; Swedish combat aircraft operating under Finnish command helped safeguard Finnish and NATO airspace; Estonia sent a navy vessel to assist with firefighting and pollution response; a Russian helicopter was suspected of briefly violating Finnish airspace during the rescue. Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said on X that "the war is spilling over into our neighbouring areas," while backing Ukraine's right to fight the aggressor. Estonia's Prime Minister Kristen Michal called the strike "another signal for the aggressor to stop."

## The Tsushima Rhyme: 1905 Was the Last Repricing of Russian Sea Power

The last time Russian sea power was broken in a way the whole world had to reprice, the fleet also came out of the Baltic. In May 1905, Japan annihilated Russia's Baltic Fleet at Tsushima after its 18,000-mile voyage to the Pacific — the first decisive defeat of a European great power by an Asian power in the modern era — and a new order in Asia began forming on the wreckage.

Nobody should stretch the parallel past its structure, but the structure rhymes. In 1905 a challenger with newer technology and better execution destroyed the assumption that Russia's fleet guaranteed its reach. In 2026 the challenger has no conventional navy at all. Ukraine's unmanned systems first achieved sea denial in the Black Sea, driving the Russian fleet from Sevastopol; the same method is now being applied to Russia's military logistics in the Baltic and the Mediterranean simultaneously. The target is not the battle fleet. It is the supply line — the thing empires actually run on. What is being repriced, as in 1905, is not one ship. It is the credibility of Russian sea power as a guarantee that Russian cargo arrives.

## What to Watch Next

### Escort Economics

Every sailing of the sealift network now prices in escorts, air defence and losses. Windward expects Russia to review maritime logistics and divert cargoes where possible.
### War-Risk Costs in EU Waters

With civilian ships sinking in Bulgarian and Romanian zones, insurers, charterers and grain traders are repricing Black Sea routes that were supposed to be the safe side of the war.
### The Kaliningrad Shuttle

Several network vessels remain active between St. Petersburg and Baltiysk. A second strike on that run converts an incident into a blockade problem.
### Allied Friction

Zelenskyy has already conceded partners were rattled. A wreck, a spill or a casualty list inside NATO waters is the scenario European capitals are now gaming.
