The Huang rally is being unwound in public. Nvidia fell another 5.2 percent in premarket trading on Wednesday to $147.27, extending Tuesday's 5.9 percent slide, as reports that Washington is preparing new tariffs on 16 trading partners hit chip stocks on three continents. Futures pointed to a third leg down for the AI trade, and the selling deepened through the morning: at 7:40 a.m. ET, S&P 500 futures were down 1.98 percent at 7,074.75, Nasdaq futures down 2.92 percent, Dow futures down 1.34 percent and Russell 2000 futures down 1.36 percent. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, jumped about 17 percent to 30.83 — back above the 30 line that marks genuine stress, not just a bad morning. Everything now waits on one number: the October PCE inflation report at 8:30 a.m. ET, the final major data release before the Federal Reserve's December 8–9 meeting.

## Nvidia: Nearly Half the Huang Rally Gone in Two Days

Monday's record gain was $39.52 a share. Tuesday gave back $9.67 of it, closing at $155.38, and Wednesday's premarket slide to $147.27 takes the two-day give-back to about $17.78 — roughly 45 percent of the rally erased in two sessions. The stock is still about 17 percent above Friday's $125.53 close, which is the bull case in one line: the reinstatement repricing has been cut nearly in half, not reversed.

The selling is sector-wide, not Nvidia-specific. Micron fell about 6 percent premarket, AMD about 6.5 percent and Marvell about 5.7 percent, Reuters reported. Anthropic, the week's other broken IPO story, slid 4.6 percent to $78.17, putting its trillion-dollar valuation in danger two sessions after it first closed below its $83 IPO price. Dell fell about 6 percent premarket even after raising its forecast.

## The New Problem: Tariff Reports Slam Chips in Asia First

The new weight on the sector is trade. Reuters reported Tuesday that the Trump administration is about to announce the results of a trade investigation into 16 economies, including Taiwan and South Korea, which could lead to new tariffs. For a chip supply chain that runs through exactly those two economies, the read-across was immediate.

Asia took the first hit overnight: South Korea's Kospi fell about 6.2 percent, with SK Hynix down about 11 percent and Samsung Electronics down about 10 percent, and SoftBank fell about 15 percent in Tokyo. That is the mirror image of Tuesday, when Asian chip names ripped higher on the Huang news while the US sold off. The two markets are now trading the same story in opposite directions on consecutive days — a sign that positioning, not fundamentals, is driving the tape.

## Washington Is Inside the Nvidia Story Now

The governance story also escalated overnight. President Trump said it is "not too late" for the United States to take an equity stake in Nvidia, noting that Intel handed over a near-10 percent stake to the government earlier this year, Investor's Business Daily reported. Trump also said a pardon for Jensen Huang is "always an option" ahead of his congressional testimony. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, meanwhile, demanded investigations into whether Chinese President Xi Jinping pressured Trump during last week's talks, and the SEC is reportedly scrutinising Nvidia's investor disclosures.

For the stock, this cuts both ways and neither way is simple: a government stake would be a political endorsement with a price attached, and a pardon discussion keeps the legal exposure on the front page. The December 1 House interview and Huang's agreed public Senate testimony are now less than a week away.

## 8:30 a.m.: the PCE Slate, With a December Hike Nearly Fully Priced

The data slate is the day's real event. Economists expect the core PCE price index to rise 0.3 percent month over month and 3.2 percent year over year in October, up from 3.1 percent in September — the plain monthly number first: inflation is expected to move up, not down. Headline PCE is expected at 3.8 percent year over year, from 3.6 percent. The second estimate of third-quarter GDP is expected to be revised to about 3.5 to 3.6 percent annualised, from 3.4 percent, alongside durable goods orders and weekly jobless claims in the same release window.

Rate futures already price a 97.8 percent chance of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the December meeting. Tuesday's consumer confidence print showed why the Fed feels it has room: the Conference Board index jumped to 103.3 in November, a 1-3/4-year high, well above the 92.4 expected. Strong growth, confident consumers and inflation ticking up is the combination that keeps the hike priced — and it is why a soft PCE print, not a hot one, is the outcome that would actually move this market.

## Commodities: Oil Gives Back the Jump, Gold Sits Near 15-Month Lows

Oil is handing back Tuesday's rally. Brent settled Tuesday at $91.40, up 2.27 percent, and WTI at $82.05, up 2.35 percent, Reuters reported — by Wednesday's early European session WTI was down about 2.25 percent near $80.20 and Brent down about 1.9 percent near $89.64, with the tariff reports and the equity selloff outweighing the previous day's bid. The EIA weekly petroleum report lands at 10:30 a.m. ET today, a day early in the holiday week.

Gold is doing the opposite of what a risk-off morning usually produces. Gold traded at $3,550.70 at 7:40 a.m. ET, down 1.13 percent on the morning, after falling about 2.2 percent on Tuesday — hovering near 15-month lows. The reason is the rate path, not sentiment: with a December hike nearly fully priced, the opportunity cost of holding bullion keeps rising, and the metal is trading as a rates asset, not a haven. Silver followed it down, near $40.20, off about 1.4 percent. Copper eased about 0.6 percent to about $5.34 a pound after falling nearly 4 percent on Tuesday on rising LME inventories and the firmer dollar. Natural gas (January) was near $3.63, down about 0.9 percent. Bitcoin slipped about 2 percent to roughly $59,000.

## Rates: Yields Fall Into the Selloff

Bonds are catching some of the bid that gold is not. The 10-year Treasury yield sat at 4.90 percent at 7:40 a.m. ET, slightly lower on the morning after dipping to about 4.85 percent in early European trading; the 2-year was roughly 4.62 percent, down about 7 basis points from Tuesday. Yields are easing into the selloff rather than rising with the tariff headlines — a risk-off pattern that sits awkwardly next to futures pricing that has a December rate hike at 97.8 percent. Both can be true for a morning: the bond bid is about today's fear, the hike pricing is about the inflation data, and the 8:30 print decides which one survives the week.

The move is global. Japan's 10-year yield fell almost 10 basis points to about 2.62 percent, and European benchmark yields were mostly 4 to 7 basis points lower.

## The Dollar: Bid Against Almost Everything

The dollar index pulled back from an overnight high near 104.5 to about 103.9 after the tariff headlines, but the greenback is still bid against nearly every major currency this morning. The euro fell to $1.0937 from $1.0967 at Tuesday's London close. Sterling declined to $1.3073 from $1.3110. The dollar advanced against the yen to ¥159.29 from ¥159.17, holding inside last Friday's ¥158.40 to ¥159.85 range. The Canadian dollar steadied near 1.4300 per US dollar, off Monday's year high of 1.4385. The Australian dollar set a new low for the year below $0.6600 — even a firmer Australian CPI print could not hold it — and the Swiss franc was the only G10 currency gaining against the dollar.

That is the full risk-off signature: stocks down, bond yields down, dollar up, and gold — the one asset that usually completes the set — left out because the fear driving this morning runs through the Fed, not around it.

## Earnings: Deere Before the Bell; Dell and Workday Already Moved

The earnings calendar is light for a holiday week, but what it lacks in volume it makes up for in signal. Deere is the day's headline report and the market's best read on farm equipment, construction demand and what tariffs are doing to a company that both manufactures in and exports from the United States. The two reports that already landed — Dell and Workday, after Tuesday's close — set the tone, and it is the same tone as the index trade: strong AI numbers are being sold anyway.

- **Deere (DE), before the open:** fiscal fourth-quarter results, with analysts expecting about $4.07 a share on revenue of about $10.4 billion. The stock closed Tuesday at $613.76, down 3.0 percent. Watch the fiscal 2027 outlook and any tariff-cost commentary — Deere is a bellwether for the industrial economy the GDP revision describes.
- **Dell (DELL), reported Tuesday after the close:** a record quarter — fiscal 2027 revenue guidance raised about 5 percent to $202 billion, with AI server revenue up 291 percent year over year to $22 billion. The stock still could not hold a bid: it fell 11.8 percent in Tuesday's regular session and its after-hours gain faded to about 1 percent. In this tape, even a 291 percent AI growth line is not enough.
- **Workday (WDAY), reported Tuesday after the close:** full-year subscription guidance raised to $9.958 billion with a 31.5 percent non-GAAP margin outlook, but fourth-quarter subscription growth guided down to 10 percent from the third quarter's 12.8 percent. The stock fell about 7 percent after hours — guidance deceleration is being punished harder than guidance raises are being rewarded.
- **Also before the open:** Li Auto (expected to lose about $0.08 a share), Kanzhun and GDS Holdings headline a small China ADR slate; Polestar, StealthGas and Diana Shipping also report. After the close, the slate is effectively empty.

## The Economic Calendar: the Whole Week Lands Today

Because Thursday is a market holiday and Friday is a half session, the week's entire data calendar has been compressed into this morning. Every release that matters lands before noon, which means the PCE print does not get the day to itself — it arrives inside a stack of growth, spending and energy data that can confirm or contradict it within the same hour.

- **8:30 a.m. ET — the main stack:** October PCE price index (headline and core), personal income and outlays, the second estimate of third-quarter GDP, October durable goods orders, weekly jobless claims and building permits, all from the BEA, Census and Labor Department.
- **10:00 a.m. ET:** October new home sales.
- **10:30 a.m. ET:** the EIA weekly petroleum status report, moved up for the holiday — the next test for an oil market that just gave back a 2 percent rally.
- **12:00 p.m. ET:** the EIA weekly natural gas storage report, also moved forward from Thursday.
- **2:00 p.m. ET:** the Federal Reserve's Beige Book, the anecdotal survey that lands two weeks before the December 8–9 meeting — the last colour the FOMC gets before it decides on the hike futures are pricing at 97.8 percent.
- **Thursday and Friday:** markets closed Thursday for Thanksgiving; Friday closes early at 1:00 p.m. ET with no major releases.

## What to Watch

- **8:30 a.m. ET:** core PCE against the 0.3 percent monthly consensus. A 0.2 percent print is the only outcome that reopens the December debate; anything at or above consensus leaves the hike priced and the chip selloff in charge.
- **The $150 line:** Nvidia's Tuesday low was $150.50 and the premarket price is already below it. Friday's $125.53 close is the level that would mark a full round trip of the reinstatement.
- **The tariff announcement itself:** reports say results on 16 economies are imminent. Taiwan and South Korea's inclusion decides whether this is a headline or a supply-chain event.
- **A short week:** markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early Friday, so Wednesday's close sets the tone on thin holiday liquidity.
