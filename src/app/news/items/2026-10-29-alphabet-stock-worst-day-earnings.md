## A record drop — and it was not the market

Alphabet's Class A shares closed Thursday at $283.82, down $41.07, or 12.64%, from Wednesday's $324.89 close. That is the stock's largest one-day percentage decrease on record, on Dow Jones data going back to the 2004 IPO, and at the intraday low of $280.36 the fall reached 13.71%. Roughly $517 billion of market value was erased in the session — the second-largest single-day loss for any U.S. company, per Dow Jones Market Data. The Nasdaq-100 fell only about 1.4% on the day: this was an Alphabet story, not a market story.

## Miss one: the profit line

Third-quarter revenue rose 25% to $127.58 billion, just below the roughly $128 billion analysts expected, and earnings per share fell to $2.12 against a $3.02 consensus — the company's first top-and-bottom-line miss in nearly four years. The biggest single drag was an $8.7 billion loss on equity securities: on the earnings call, management attributed it primarily to an unrealized mark-down of Alphabet's stake in SpaceX, which cut EPS by $0.55. Even excluding that paper loss, EPS of about $2.67 still missed. Net income fell to $26.5 billion from $35.0 billion a year earlier, and operating income of $40.58 billion, up 30% year over year, also came in below the roughly $43.2 billion consensus.

## Miss two: search slowed

Google Search revenue grew 12% to $63.35 billion, a clear deceleration from 17% growth in the second quarter, and below estimates. Management pointed to currency moves, softer retail ad spending in Asia, and lower monetization rates on AI Mode queries — the AI-powered search format earns less per query than the results page it is replacing. YouTube advertising also missed, at $11.21 billion. As VanEck's Jamie Hannah put it to Reuters, Alphabet's traditional ad business "has been impacted by the very thing which is the AI boom."

## Miss three: the cash

Capital spending hit $51.36 billion in the quarter against $42.3 billion of operating cash flow, pushing free cash flow to negative $9.07 billion — analysts had expected a positive $2.7 billion. To fund the buildout, Alphabet issued about $12.4 billion of common stock and raised $28.7 billion of debt during the quarter; long-term debt has climbed to $125.1 billion from $21.6 billion at the start of the year. The 2026 capex guide was held at $195–205 billion, and 2027 spending was guided only as "to increase significantly" — the company gave no figure; the roughly $300 billion number in circulation is an analyst estimate, not guidance.

## What the market ignored

Google Cloud more than doubled: revenue up 103% to $30.71 billion, well past the $28.8 billion expected, with operating income of $11.1 billion and a 36.2% margin. On any other day that print carries the stock. On Thursday it was a footnote to the three misses above — a quarter where the growth engine worked and the stock still had its worst day ever.
