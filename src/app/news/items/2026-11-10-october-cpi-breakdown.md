NEW YORK, Nov 10 — The consumer price index rose **0.4% in October from September**, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Tuesday, after a 0.6% rise in September. Core prices — excluding food and energy — rose **0.3%**, the same pace as in August and September. The CPI-U index level stands at 337.511.

## Where the increase is coming from

- **Shelter:** +0.3%, accounting for over 30% of the monthly all-items increase. Rent rose 0.2% and owners' equivalent rent 0.3%.
- **Food:** +0.5% — food at home +0.4%, food away from home +0.5%. Meats, poultry, fish and eggs rose 0.5%; fruits and vegetables and dairy and related products each rose 0.6%.
- **Services:** recreation +0.8%, transportation services +0.8%, motor vehicle insurance +0.7%, lodging away from home +0.6%, medical care +0.3%, education +0.3%.
- **Energy:** +0.7% on the month — gasoline +1.0%, electricity +0.9%, fuel oil +2.0%, partly offset by piped gas service at −0.8%.

## Where it has already cooled

- Energy's monthly pace fell from **+5.2% in September to +0.7%**; gasoline's fell from +9.1% to +1.0%.
- Used cars and trucks swung from +0.7% in September to **−1.2%**, and are down 3.3% year over year.
- Core goods (commodities less food and energy commodities) rose just 0.2%, and the communication index fell 0.1%.
- Shelter's +0.3% is down from +0.4% in September and +0.6% in April.

## Energy, year over year

BLS did not publish a 12-month change for the energy index — October 2025 survey data was never collected during the lapse in appropriations, so most year-over-year rates do not exist this month, for headline or core. The one energy component with a published 12-month figure is **gasoline: +37.4% year over year**. The only other published 12-month changes in the release are new vehicles (+0.7%) and used cars and trucks (−3.3%).

The November CPI is scheduled for release on December 10 at 8:30 a.m. ET. Producer prices for October land Friday, November 13.
