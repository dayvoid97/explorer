## What happened

Tesla unveiled its redesigned second-generation Roadster on October 15 at SpaceX's test site in McGregor, Texas — and it flew. The prototype drove up an inverted ramp on underbody suction fans, spun through the air on cold-gas thrusters, landed itself, then leapt over a Cybercab, Cybertruck, Semi, Model Y, and Model 3 while in motion. A live run clocked **0-60 mph in 0.81 seconds**. No human was driving — Musk said that choice was meant to "avoid killing anyone."

The hardware: 10 cold-gas thrusters fed by a ~600-liter compressed-air tank the car recharges itself. Tesla claims 230+ mph top speed and 300+ miles of range.

![Tesla Roadster: 0.81 seconds, 10 thrusters, one flying car](https://financialgurkha.com/news/tesla-roadster-highlight-1.jpg)

## Three trims, three price tags

| Trim | Pitch | Reservation |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | Road-ready, 0-60 under 1.6s | **$50,000** |
| Thrust | Race-ready, rear thrusters, 0-60 under 0.9s | **$250,000** |
| Rocket | Air-ready, single seat, 10 thrusters — not street legal | Invite only |

Production is slated for **2028** — eight years after the car's original delivery target. The Rocket trim, Tesla says, is not approved for public roads. Tesla's video of the stunt passed 54 million views on X in under a day.

## The stock: pop, then fade

Here's the part that matters for shareholders. Overnight, with Nasdaq closed, crypto venues became the first place to bet on the news: Hyperliquid's TSLA perpetual contract jumped ~4.9% to ~$378 on volume that leapt from $2.8M to $47M in an hour. Tesla shares were up ~4.5% premarket.

Then the regular session opened — and the pop faded. TSLA traded down to **$356.68, -1.6%** on the day, underperforming a broadly higher market. At ~$357, the stock sits ~28% below its 52-week high of $498.83, worth about $1.45 trillion at a trailing P/E near 340.

![TSLA: +4.5% premarket, -1.6% by the close](https://financialgurkha.com/news/tesla-roadster-highlight-2.jpg)

## The rate backdrop

Long-term rates are doing Tesla no favors. The 10-year Treasury sits around **5.09%** and the 30-year near **5.44%** — both just off three-week lows, with money markets pricing three more Fed hikes over the next year. At a 340x trailing multiple, Tesla is one of the most duration-sensitive stocks in the market: every basis point of long-end yield is a headwind for the valuation. The average 30-year mortgage, for reference, is 7.46%.

Tesla reports Q3 earnings on **October 21** — five days from now. That's when the conversation moves from flying cars back to deliveries, margins, and guidance.

## Our take

The Roadster demo is a genuine engineering flex — nobody else is putting cold-gas thrusters on a production-intent car. But the tape is telling its own story: a 4.5% premarket pop that fully reversed into a down day says the market filed this under "spectacle," not "earnings driver." With the 10-year above 5%, a 340 P/E, and earnings five days out, Tesla doesn't need a flying car right now — it needs a quarter that justifies the multiple. Watch October 21.
