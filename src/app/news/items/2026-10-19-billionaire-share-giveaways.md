## Two pledges, 48 hours

"Next year, I will give one of my SpaceX shares to every kid in America," Elon Musk wrote on X at 3:52 a.m. ET on Monday. "This will be the first of many."

It landed one day after AI rival Anthropic announced it would commit **5% of its shares** to a new "Anthropic Public Benefit Fund." Musk's public reply to Anthropic on Sunday: "I recommend giving the shares directly to the public. This is what I will be doing."

Two of the most valuable private-market-born companies in the world, two equity giveaways, one weekend. Whether this is a genuine philanthropic turn or the AI industry's most expensive PR campaign is the question the numbers can help answer.

## The Musk pledge: ~$10 billion, ~70 million kids

SpaceX closed Friday at **$146.03** a share. The U.S. Census Bureau counts roughly **70–73 million** Americans under 18. One share each puts the giveaway at roughly **$10–10.7 billion** — about **1% of Musk's roughly $900 billion net worth**.

The share count works. Musk holds voting power over about **6.42 billion SpaceX shares**, just under half the company, per the company's Schedule 13G. As Barron's notes, roughly 850 million of those are Class A shares he could distribute without ceding control — he's unlikely to hand out the Class B stock carrying 10 votes per share.

What Musk did not say: how the shares get distributed, who exactly is eligible, or when. SpaceX did not respond to press requests for comment. The most likely rails already exist — the federal **Trump Accounts** program, under which the Treasury is auto-enrolling tens of millions of children into investment accounts. New Treasury rules let wealthy donors contribute stock while avoiding capital-gains taxes, per the Journal. SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell already walked this path: in July she pledged about **$320 million** in SpaceX stock to investment accounts for 2 million children aged 11–17 through the program.

Timing matters. SpaceX reports third-quarter earnings **Tuesday** — FactSet expects 14 cents a share on $12.9 billion in sales. The stock, down more than 10% last week in the tech selloff, was up about 2.7% in early Monday trading near $150.

## The Anthropic fund: 5% now, more as it grows

Anthropic's structure is the more engineered of the two. On October 18, the company announced the **Anthropic Public Benefit Fund**, committing **5% of its shares** at launch — subject to stockholder approval — with a "valuation escalator": the board may add another **2% for every 50% increase** in the company's value above an undisclosed baseline.

The fund will be a **wholly owned LLC**, not a 501(c)(3) charity — a deliberate choice, the company says, for flexibility between large donations to nonprofits and **direct distributions to specific populations**. Anthropic pledges none of the equity will be used for lobbying or political giving, in the U.S. or abroad.

The stated purpose is AI's labor-market fallout. Anthropic has already committed **$200 million** to its Economic Futures Research Fund studying the issue; the new fund is the "bolder approach" for a faster-moving AI economy. Combined with the founders' pledge to donate **80% of their personal equity** and an employee matching program about half the staff uses, Anthropic says roughly **20% of its shares** will be dedicated to public purposes.

The calendar explains the urgency: Anthropic was valued at **$965 billion** in a $65 billion round in May, and is reportedly eyeing an IPO as early as **November** — which would make it the largest IPO ever by a public benefit corporation. The fund locks in the giveaway before public-market shareholders get a vote.

## The playbook isn't new

**Patagonia, 2022.** Yvon Chouinard gave away the entire $3 billion company: 2% of voting stock to the Patagonia Purpose Trust, 98% to the Holdfast Collective nonprofit. "Earth is now our only shareholder." Every dollar of profit after reinvestment funds climate action, in perpetuity. The key difference: Chouinard surrendered the company. Musk and Anthropic are donating slices while keeping control.

**The Giving Pledge, 2010.** Buffett and Gates's pledge has drawn 240+ billionaires committing the majority of their wealth to philanthropy. Mostly cash and estate commitments — equity-for-the-public at this scale, pre-IPO, is rarer.

**Trump Accounts, 2026.** The federal program auto-enrolling minors, with a $1,000 government contribution for children born 2025–2028, is the distribution infrastructure both Musk's and Shotwell's pledges appear designed to ride on.

## Why now

Follow the incentives. AI companies are facing real public pushback — data-center fights, job-displacement anxiety, questions about whether AI debt can pay for itself. Anthropic's own framing admits the tension: "It is common practice for tech companies to offer escalating compensation awards to their executives. We are instead directing that equity to the public benefit." Donating equity ahead of an IPO also converts would-be executive comp into goodwill at pre-public valuations.

For Musk, the pledge arrives the morning after he publicly needled a rival, the day before SpaceX earnings, and weeks before the November midterms he's spending heavily on. It also costs him, in relative terms, very little: 1% of his net worth, in shares he can spare without losing control, with a likely tax-efficient delivery mechanism.

## The fine print

Musk's version is a social-media post with no mechanism, no eligibility criteria, and no date — "next year" and "the first of many" is the entire plan so far. Anthropic's is a real corporate commitment, but the escalator baseline is undisclosed, the extra 2% tranches are at the board's discretion (not automatic), stockholders still have to approve the whole thing, and the company hasn't said how shares become money for beneficiaries or when distributions begin.

The generous read: two companies built on transformative technology are putting real equity — not press-release cash — toward the public before they're forced to. The skeptical read: $10 billion sounds enormous until it's 1% of a fortune, and 5% sounds structural until the baseline is a secret.

## What to watch

- **Tuesday:** SpaceX Q3 earnings — does Musk elaborate on the giveaway on the call?
- **November:** Anthropic's expected IPO — the stockholder vote on the 5% commitment happens before or around it.
- **The mechanics:** whether Trump Accounts becomes the default rail for billionaire stock gifts, and what the Treasury's final donor rules allow.
- **"The first of many":** Musk's closing line is a promise of sequels. The AI wealth-transfer era may just be getting started.
