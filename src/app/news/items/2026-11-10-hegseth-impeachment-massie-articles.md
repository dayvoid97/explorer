WASHINGTON, Nov 10 — Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) introduced articles of impeachment against Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth on Monday, reading the 34-page resolution on the House floor on the first day of the post-midterm lame-duck session. The resolution is privileged, which forces House action by **Wednesday, November 11**: leadership can move to table it, refer it to committee, or hold a floor vote on the articles themselves.

If any article passes by simple majority, Hegseth would be the first Pentagon chief impeached by the House since War Secretary William Belknap in 1876. The case would then go to the Senate for trial, where conviction and removal need two-thirds — considered unlikely with Republicans holding 53 of 100 seats.

## The eight articles

Five are war-powers counts. Article I charges waging war on Iran in violation of the War Powers Resolution — U.S. forces introduced into hostilities on February 28, 2026 with no declaration of war, no statutory authorization and no national emergency — and quotes the March 17 resignation letter of counterterrorism director Joe Kent: "Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation." Article II charges continuing hostilities after both chambers passed a concurrent resolution directing the President to remove U.S. forces from Iran. Article III charges continuing past the 60-day termination clock. Article VIII charges an unlawful war in Yemen.

The remaining three allege: dismantling congressionally mandated civilian-harm protections — cutting over 90% of the mitigation staff and firing the senior Judge Advocates General — ahead of the February 28 strike on the Shajarah Tayyebeh Elementary School in Minab, Iran, which the resolution says killed and injured more than 200 civilians; directing strikes that killed at least 243 people aboard alleged drug-smuggling vessels in the Caribbean Sea and eastern Pacific; suppressing free speech in the effort to punish Sen. Mark Kelly over a video about refusing illegal orders; and capturing Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in a January raid without congressional authorization. Each article closes with the formula that Hegseth "has demonstrated that he will remain a threat to the Constitution if allowed to remain in office."

## The politics

Massie has **no co-sponsors**. "We're not indicting him for being bad at his job," he said. "The argument here is that he's broken laws and he's violated the Constitution." He is betting on Republican members who lost re-election and now vote unconstrained; congressional sources told Reuters the resolution may have the simple majority to pass the current House, which Republicans hold 218–214. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, the presumptive next Speaker, is "a hard yes." Speaker Mike Johnson opposes it, saying impeachment "in the middle of hostilities abroad" would be "a terrible thing for our allies and our enemies to see." Rep. Warren Davidson (R-OH) called it "a mistake."

This is a reintroduction: Massie first moved the same articles in September, but the resolution expired when Johnson sent the House into its pre-election recess early. Massie himself lost his Republican primary to a Trump-backed challenger and leaves office in January.

## The response

Hegseth is defiant. In a video last week he dismissed the "sham articles of impeachment" from a "disgruntled, defeated congressman," and at a rare Pentagon press conference on Tuesday — his first briefing-room questions since May — he said: "They will fail again, and if standing with President Trump puts me in that company, I'll take that company every day of the week." Asked Monday whether he had the Republican votes to beat it, he said, "We're not worried about that."

President Trump is fully behind him. On CBS's 60 Minutes on Sunday: "They want to impeach him. Very unfair. We had two wars, we won both." To reporters Monday: "I'll stand by him. He won two wars. He was in charge." Trump attacked Massie personally as a "stone-cold loser." Massie's answer to Hegseth's "defeated congressman" line, via NPR: "Well, he's right about one thing — I'm a defeated congressman. I'm not disgruntled." He also urged Trump to replace Hegseth now, while a replacement can still be confirmed: "Impeachment would be much gentler than what's in store."

*This brief reports the resolution, the stated positions of the parties, and the procedural status. The allegations in the articles are the filer's charges, not findings.*
