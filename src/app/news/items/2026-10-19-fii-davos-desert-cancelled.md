## The desert Davos is off

Saudi Arabia has postponed the **Future Investment Initiative** — the "Davos in the Desert" — from October 26–29, 2026 to **2027**. The tenth edition, themed "The Power of Legacy," will have to wait for safer skies.

The reason isn't diplomatic. It's ballistic. On **October 10**, a Houthi missile struck Riyadh's **King Khalid International Airport**, killing 12 to 18 people and injuring more than 300. Days earlier, Houthi strikes hit Abha airport; by October 13, drones and missiles had set part of **Aramco's headquarters** ablaze. A projectile struck the southern city of Najran on Sunday, injuring six more.

FII Institute CEO Princess Maha bint Mishari Al Saud framed it as quality control: the milestone tenth edition must deliver "the full scale, quality and impact" partners expect. The Wall Street version is blunter — per the Journal, bankers at JPMorgan and Citigroup were already canceling meetings, and teams around **Jamie Dimon** and **Jane Fraser** were debating whether their CEOs should fly into a war zone. When your airport is the target, the conference is the casualty.

## The money at stake

FII isn't just a conference. Launched in **2017** with backing from the **Public Investment Fund**, it is the shop window of **Vision 2030** — Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's plan to turn the oil exporter into a hub for finance, tourism, and tech. NEOM itself was unveiled at the first FII.

The speaker list for this year tells you what was lost: Dimon, Fraser, Goldman Sachs' David Solomon, BlackRock's Larry Fink, Blackstone's Stephen Schwarzman, Standard Chartered's Bill Winters. These are the people MBS needs to fund the diversification. Now the 2026 deal calendar has a hole in it — and per the Journal, mounting war costs are already straining the kingdom's finances. Postponing the flagship investor gathering is the opposite of the signal Vision 2030 needs.

## Why the real Davos is still top G

Here's the contrast the kingdom can't buy its way out of. The **World Economic Forum** has met in **Davos, Switzerland, since 1971** — 55 years of war, oil shocks, financial crises, terror waves, and a pandemic. It has never been cancelled because someone started shooting.

That's the moat, and it's three layers deep:

**Neutral ground.** Switzerland doesn't take sides, so everyone shows up — rivals included. Riyadh, for all its money, is a party to the region's wars. You can't host the world's neutral meeting ground while you're exchanging missile fire.

**Continuity compounds.** Fifty-five annual meetings built the Rolodex, the rituals, the muscle memory. FII had nine editions. Davos has generations. When CEOs allocate one "must-attend" week a year, incumbency wins.

**Geography is destiny.** A mountain town in a neutral country can't be reached by a Houthi missile. A conference center in Riyadh can. Security isn't a footnote for a global summit — it's the product. Davos sells safety; this week, Riyadh couldn't.

None of this diminishes what FII achieved — it genuinely became a must-attend for global finance. But the postponement proves the point: money rents prestige, neutrality owns it.

## The Middle East, right now

The FII cancellation is a symptom. Here's the state of play:

**The war.** The United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran on **February 28**, and the region hasn't stood down since. The Houthis — Iran-aligned, Yemen-based — sat out the first months, then tore up the collapsed Saudi-Houthi truce and went on the offensive in September.

**The escalation ladder.** October has been the worst month: the airport massacre on the 10th, Aramco burning on the 13th, a Houthi surface-to-air missile striking a Saudi Eurofighter on the 14th (the second Saudi jet they've hit, after an F-15 in September), and a Saudi helicopter downed on the 18th. Near-daily missile, drone, and air strikes both ways.

**The players.** On October 4, Yemen's internationally recognized leader Rashad al-Alimi announced operations to retake Houthi territory. The **UAE** just resumed salary payments for Yemeni government-aligned forces. **Egypt's** President Sisi offered Saudi Arabia defensive military aid. Iran's foreign minister has offered to mediate — while hardliners in Tehran threaten escalation, a split that tells you Iran itself isn't sure what it wants.

**The oil bid.** Brent is holding near **$110** — the Strait of Hormuz overhang plus the Aramco strikes keep a risk premium in every barrel. The 2019 Abqaiq attack halved Saudi processing capacity and spiked Brent 14% in a day; this time the market is pricing the *threat* of the next one, not just the last one.

**The calendar.** The US-Israeli war already wiped out the Gulf's early-2026 conference circuit in the UAE and Qatar. Now Riyadh. The region's business calendar is being written by missile trajectories.

## What to watch

- **FII 2027 dates** — "very soon," organizers say. The real signal is whether the CEOs rebook.
- **Airlines** — if major carriers formally suspend Riyadh service, the economic damage compounds fast.
- **Oil** — any Houthi hit on production infrastructure (not just offices) reprices crude violently.
- **The US midterms** — November's elections will shape whether Washington escalates or deals.
- **Davos, January** — expect the WEF to look more indispensable than ever, with the desert Davos as the cautionary tale.
