_This brief summarises an investigation by the Financial Times and its reporting partners. Every finding below is the FT's reporting, credited as such throughout. Financial Gurkha has not seen the leaked files._

The Financial Times reported Tuesday that two whistleblowers leaked more than 15 million internal Deutsche Bank documents to journalists, in what the FT calls the largest cache of internal files ever leaked from inside a bank. The documents — emails, internal probes, board minutes, compliance files and employee chat logs dating back to the early 2010s — were examined by more than 200 journalists from dozens of outlets, including Der Spiegel, Le Monde and The New York Times, in a joint investigation the FT titles "Inside Deutsche Bank." The project was co-ordinated by Barracuda Media, a South African not-for-profit, with technical support from the Data and Research Center in Germany.

Deutsche Bank shares fell as much as 6 percent in Frankfurt on Tuesday after the first stories appeared, before paring the drop to about 4 percent, the FT reported. Reuters, in its morning markets commentary, likewise reported the shares down more than 5 percent in early trading.

## What the FT says the files show

According to the FT, the files expose systemic failures, incompetence and broken promises to reform at the German lender, along with the secrets of some of its billionaire and oligarch clients. The investigation also reveals accounting tricks and financial disclosures that, in the FT's characterisation, border on deception.

Much of the reporting centres on chief executive Christian Sewing, the insider who took over in 2018 and is widely credited with bringing the scandal-prone bank back from the brink to record profits. In public, the FT says, Sewing promised to draw a line under the scandals. In private, the files show a chief executive who pushed for greater risk-taking, railed at compliance and kept some of the problems of the bank from shareholders, according to the FT. Partner coverage of the investigation describes internal messages in which Sewing urged colleagues to take on more risk up to internally agreed limits, and documents showing the bank continued to pursue business with about 50 companies it ranked among its "most sensitive clients" by exposure to the Kremlin and financial crime.

## The stories the FT published Tuesday

The FT published several strands of the investigation on Tuesday:

- **Russia.** Deutsche plotted an expansion of its Russia business under Sewing, over-ruling the fears of its compliance team about the Kremlin-connected clientele of the bank as geopolitical storm clouds gathered, the FT reported.
- **Trump's taxes.** The bank accidentally leaked the tax statements of Donald Trump during his first presidential term and kept the mistake secret from the White House, with Sewing briefed on its handling of the crisis, according to the FT. Partner reports on the investigation say the tax document was sent to the accountant of another client in 2017.
- **Epstein's money.** Deutsche processed almost $600 million of suspicious payments linked to Jeffrey Epstein, a far higher figure than previously known, the FT reported. The suspicious activity reports reviewed in the investigation put the figure at $582 million, against a previously reported figure of more than $250 million.
- **Compliance.** The FT describes a catalogue of compliance deficiencies, governance weaknesses, ineptitude and technology failings, including what it calls a confession to regulators that Deutsche was in effect "operating outside of law and order."

## The whistleblowers

The FT reports that the bank's relationship with oligarchs and Russian money particularly motivated the two whistleblowers to copy the cache and share it with Indian journalist Josy Joseph and South African journalist Khadija Sharife. In a statement reported by the FT, the whistleblowers said they acted in the "public interest" to expose the behaviour of the bank, including "issues such as accounts held by Russian oligarchs," and that "the bank needs a complete overhaul in management and culture." Partner outlets report the whistleblowers also provided material to the US Department of Justice, the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network and the UK's HM Revenue and Customs, according to their lawyer.

## Deutsche Bank responds

Deutsche Bank told the FT that the issues raised by the leak "date back many years and in some cases as much as two decades," and that where relevant the matters "were reviewed at the time and were discussed with regulators." The bank said: "We are a different bank today. Under Christian Sewing's leadership, Deutsche Bank has gone through a fundamental transformation." It said €7 billion was invested to improve controls between 2019 and 2025, and that its progress has been recognised by regulators, the market and clients. Deutsche chair Alexander Wynaendts separately backed Sewing, in a statement reported by Reuters.

The backdrop, as the FT recounts it, is a bank that entered a deferred prosecution agreement with the US Department of Justice in 2021, has been raided by the German authorities three times this year alone, and has paid €18.6 billion since 2012 to settle alleged conduct violations and litigation spanning market manipulation, Russian money laundering, weak controls, mis-selling and misrepresentation.

## What comes next

The FT says further articles this week will cover secret payments to a Qatari royal and weaknesses in the systems meant to prevent money laundering at Deutsche, which handles more than €1 trillion of international transfers every day. Reuters notes it has not been able to independently verify the consortium's reports.
