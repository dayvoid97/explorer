Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed, and enlarged the deal to 980 million shares — raising $81.31 billion and making it the largest stock-market debut on record, ahead of SpaceX's offering in June. The shares began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on Thursday, November 19, under the ticker ANT — and opened at $111.50, 34% above the offer price, valuing the company at about $2 trillion fully diluted at the first print, according to Reuters.

The price values Anthropic at about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, including stock options and restricted stock, and about $1.1 trillion on shares outstanding, according to Reuters. That is the number the morning's guide was waiting for: at $78, the top of the range, the fully diluted figure was about $1.405 trillion. The $83 price adds roughly $5 a share on top of that — and the upsizing adds far more to the raise itself.

## The final math

The offering grew by about 19% from the marketed size:

- **980 million Class A shares**, up from 825 million. About 956.4 million are new shares sold by Anthropic itself; about 23.6 million come from selling stockholders, the seven co-founders, whose proceeds go to them, not the company.
- **$81.31 billion gross** on the 980 million shares at $83. Of that, about $79.4 billion goes to Anthropic from its new shares.
- **The greenshoe grew with the deal:** the underwriters can buy up to 147 million more shares within 30 days, up from 123.75 million in the marketed terms.
- **The $26 billion private placement closes alongside**, at the same $83 price, in non-voting Class N shares: Google $10 billion, Nvidia $10 billion, AMD $2.5 billion, and $1 billion each from Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft, with TSMC at $0.5 billion.
- **Close is expected Friday, November 20**, subject to customary conditions. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead the 22-bank syndicate.

At the marketed 825 million shares, $83 would have meant about $68.5 billion. The extra 155 million shares Anthropic added are worth about $12.9 billion at the offer price — the company took the demand and turned it into cash, not just a higher headline.

## What $83 does to the Figma parallel

This morning's guide built the Figma lesson around a price set at or above the range. The final price makes the parallel sharper, not weaker. Figma priced at $33, opened at $85 and closed its first day at $115.50, up 250% — then fell about 85% from its $142.92 peak to about $21.75, below its IPO price, while the business kept growing. Anthropic starts its public life the same way Figma did: a heavily oversubscribed book, a price above the range, and a float that is a small fraction of the company.

The difference is the starting point. Figma's pop was measured from $33. Anthropic's first trade was measured from $83 — already 6.4% above the top of the range, and already carrying a $1.5 trillion fully diluted valuation. The opening pop was a pop on top of a pop: $111.50 at the first trade, 34% above the offer price. The guide's warning stands: the first print is the market's excitement, not its verdict. The verdict arrives over quarters — in reported revenue, in margins, and at every lock-up window, the first of which opens on the first trading day.

## The numbers behind the price

The filing's economics have not changed since the morning guide, and they are the reason the price held above the range. Third-quarter revenue was $16.96 billion, up 47% from the second quarter, on a run-rate of about $81 billion as of October 31 — up $10 billion in a single month. Anthropic had 11,900 customers paying at least $100,000 a year as of September 30, against more than 1,500 at the end of 2025.

The other side of the same filing is the cost. Anthropic spent about $9 billion on compute in the first half of 2026 and another $5 billion in the third quarter, and carries about $900 billion in compute spending commitments. It reported an operating loss of $567 million in the third quarter, against operating income of $974 million in the second, and a net loss of $23.3 billion. Its prospectus warns that some of its computing providers depend on third-party financing — and that if that financing becomes unavailable or more costly, contracted capacity may be delayed, repriced, reduced or not delivered.

That warning is now a Federal Reserve concern as well as a company disclosure: the minutes of the Fed's October meeting, released today, record that most participants observed AI-related debt growing rapidly through "complex and opaque financing structures." The FOMC brief carries that story in full.

## What happens next

Thursday's opening auction set the first public price at 11:48 a.m., after the book balanced: $111.50 — judged against the $83 offer price, and above even the grey-market contracts that changed hands near $104 earlier in the week, a sentiment gauge from a thin market, not a forecast. Reuters reported the shares last trading at $108.72 in the early afternoon. The first day's close is the next marker.

After the debut, the calendar from the morning guide takes over: the underwriters' 30-day option window, the staggered lock-up releases running from the first trading day to November 2027, and Anthropic's first earnings report as a public company for the December quarter.
