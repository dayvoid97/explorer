NEW YORK, Nov 9 — U.S. stocks rallied hard on Monday, clawing back most of last week's losses in the S&P 500's strongest session since April 8. A slide in oil prices cooled inflation fears, chip stocks rebounded from their worst week in more than a year, and every one of the 11 S&P 500 sectors closed higher. The one big name that missed the rally was Nvidia, down 4.27% after its board opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang.

The S&P 500 opened at 7,422.24 — which was also its session low — climbed to a high of 7,577.39, and closed 27 points under that high (session data). Breadth matched the index move: 418 S&P 500 stocks rose against 80 decliners, and advancers beat decliners by about 4.5 to 1 on the NYSE, Reuters reported. The index remains 3.4% below the all-time high it set in October (AP).

## What drove it

**Oil fell, and took inflation fears with it.** U.S. crude fell about 4.3% to roughly $87 a barrel and Brent fell about 5.6% to about $92.80, after President Trump said the U.S. was unlikely to renew attacks on Iran while talks progress. Brent had been above $112 at the start of the month. Cheaper fuel lifted airlines — United gained 6.78% and Delta 5.28% — while Exxon Mobil and Chevron slipped against the tape.

**Chips bounced.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose about 6.9% after falling more than 12% last week (Reuters). AMD jumped 13.26% after an Oppenheimer upgrade, Broadcom gained nearly 9%, and chip equipment names including Applied Materials and Lam Research rose more than 10%. Industrials and consumer discretionary were the top S&P sectors, each up more than 3%, and Caterpillar led the Dow, up 7.21%.

**The rebound had a backdrop.** Last week's selloff — the S&P 500's biggest weekly drop since April 2025 — followed the Democrats' sweeping midterm victory and the concern that new regulation could dent the durability of the AI trade (Reuters). Monday's session recovered most of that ground in a day, with the volatility index down 22% to 19.92.

## The exception: Nvidia

Nvidia fell 4.27% to $178.67 and was the only decliner in the semiconductor index. The stock dropped more than 8% in the morning to its lowest intraday level since March 31 before recovering part of the loss; the decline erased roughly $192 billion of market value at the close (CNN). The board's investigation, announced Sunday, examines reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort; the board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and Huang remains CEO. ## Cross-asset close

- **Treasury yields:** the 10-year was little changed at about 4.86% — the rally was not a falling-rates story.
- **Gold:** December gold rose about 1.8% to about $4,147 an ounce; silver gained 2.62% to $53.31.
- **Dollar:** the dollar index fell 0.61% to 101.67.
- **Inflation expectations:** the New York Fed's October survey put median one-year expectations at 3.9%, essentially unchanged from September.

## What is next

The October consumer price index lands Tuesday at 8:30 a.m. ET — economists surveyed expect a 0.3% monthly rise in headline CPI and 0.2% in core. Producer prices follow on Friday. And the stock at the center of Monday's exception reports earnings on November 17: Nvidia.
