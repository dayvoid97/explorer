## The print

Yesterday we reported that missiles had cancelled "Davos in the Desert" — [Saudi Arabia postponed its flagship FII conference](https://financialgurkha.com/news/2026-10-19-fii-davos-desert-cancelled) after a missile hit Riyadh's international airport. The market angle in that piece was an oil bid building under the escalation. Today it printed.

**Brent crude touched $111.75 a barrel on Tuesday, its highest since May**, and held above $110 through the European session (Reuters). The expiring November WTI contract spiked as high as $110 intraday — an expiry-day squeeze on top of the geopolitics. Both benchmarks sit above $100.

## Claimed vs confirmed

Precision matters here, because the loudest claims are also the least verified.

**Claimed by the Houthis (not confirmed):** on Monday the group said it launched attacks on an Aramco site in **Yanbu**, the **Shaybah oilfield**, **Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport** and **Abha airport**. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the claims, and Reuters could not independently verify them.

**Confirmed:** this follows a week in which a strike on Riyadh's airport killed **13 people**, Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry confirmed a limited fire at Aramco's Dhahran headquarters after an attack there, and Saudi-led forces intercepted a ballistic missile fired toward Riyadh. On Monday, a Houthi attack on a government security training camp in Yemen's Shabwa province killed at least **73 people** and injured more than 200 (provincial media office; Reuters verified video from the scene).

## The shipping dimension

The premium is not only about Saudi soil. Ship-tracker **Kpler counts at least 18 vessels reportedly attacked over the past two weeks** in the Gulf region, and the Houthis have seized control of the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint at the Red Sea's mouth. ING's read, in a Tuesday note: oil prices "remain well-supported" because "the market is pricing in a significant risk premium" — one that "would likely erode slowly" only if flows continue uninhibited, and "clearly, there are no signs of this yet."

## The strain is showing in the kingdom

Saudi air travel is already under severe strain, with **more than 45% of departures from the kingdom's airports cancelled since October 8** (Cirium, via Reuters). The Saudi benchmark TASI fell 1.1% on Tuesday, and organizers formally postponed the FII conference to next year on Monday — the cancellation we reported yesterday, now official.

## What it means from here

A $110-plus Brent into next week's **October 28 FOMC** meeting tightens the Fed's corner through the oldest channel there is: energy into headline inflation. The escalation that emptied Riyadh's conference calendar is now setting the price of the world's most important commodity — and the premium, per the analysts watching flows, fades only when the ships move freely again.
