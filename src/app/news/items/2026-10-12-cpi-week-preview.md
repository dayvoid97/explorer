## What happened

Nothing yet — this one is a preview, because Wednesday morning is when it counts. At 8:30am ET, the US government publishes its September inflation report (the CPI). Early expectations put headline inflation around 3.6% for the year, up a touch from 3.4% — but the number insiders watch most, "core" monthly inflation, is expected to cool from 0.3% to 0.2%.

Why does one report matter so much? Because the Federal Reserve just raised interest rates in mid-September — its first hike since 2023 — and its next meeting is October 27–28. This inflation data is the freshest evidence policymakers will have when they decide whether to hike again.

## What it means for you

Interest rates are the price of money, and this report helps set that price for everyone. If inflation comes in cool (0.2% or lower on the monthly core read), the Fed can probably sit still — good news for mortgages, car loans, and credit cards down the line. If it comes in hot, expect talk of another hike, and borrowing stays expensive longer.

One friendly reminder: a single month doesn't make a trend. Economists will squint at this report, argue about it for a day, and then wait for the next one. You don't need to refresh your banking app on Wednesday — but you'll feel this number in your wallet over the coming months either way.
