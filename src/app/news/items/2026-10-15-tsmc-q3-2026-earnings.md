## What happened

Taiwan Semiconductor — the world's largest dedicated chip foundry, the company that manufactures the silicon inside Nvidia's AI accelerators, Apple's iPhones, and AMD's data-center chips — reported third-quarter results this morning that beat on every line. And the stock fell roughly 3%.

| Metric | Q3 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | NT$1,494.2B (**$46.72B**) | **+50.9%** YoY / +17.6% QoQ |
| Net income | NT$763.1B (**$23.9B**) | **+68.7%** YoY — a record |
| Diluted EPS | NT$29.43 ($4.60/ADR) | +68.7% YoY |
| Gross margin | 66.4% | vs 59.5% a year ago |
| Operating margin | 58.7% | — |
| Net margin | 51.1% | — |

Revenue beat the company's own guidance ($44.6–45.8B), and net income topped the LSEG analyst consensus (NT$746.3B) by about 2%. It was the sixth straight quarter of record profit.

## The number behind the number

Two details matter more than the headline. First, the **2-nanometer ramp**: the newest node jumped from 3% of wafer revenue in Q2 to **8%** in Q3, with 3nm at 33% and 5nm at 28% — advanced nodes (7nm and below) now account for **79%** of wafer revenue. Second, **high-performance computing hit 68% of total revenue**, up from 57% a year earlier, while smartphones shrank to 21%. TSMC is no longer a phone-chip company that does AI on the side. It's an AI-chip company.

CEO C.C. Wei's line from the call says it all: AI demand **still exceeds TSMC's supply capability**. The company is working to narrow the gap and is reportedly evaluating expansion beyond Arizona, including Texas.

For Q4, management guided revenue of **$52.3–54.3B** with gross margins of 65–67% — another sequential step up, supported by the steep 2nm ramp.

## Our take

This is the foundry pulse of the entire AI trade: when the company that makes everyone else's chips says demand exceeds supply for the sixth straight record quarter, the AI capex cycle is not over — whatever the Nasdaq did today. The selloff looks like classic "priced for perfection" profit-taking, not a fundamental crack.

One valuation note: with the 10-year Treasury at ~5.06% and the Fed funds rate at 3.75–4%, the discount rate on future cash flows is doing real work against even great earnings. TSMC's margins (66% gross, 51% net) are the envy of manufacturing — but at these rates, the market demands the growth be not just real, but perpetual. Today's reaction suggests it's starting to ask whether it is.
