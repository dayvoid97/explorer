## What happened

Taiwan Semiconductor — the world's largest dedicated chip foundry, the company that manufactures the silicon inside Nvidia's AI accelerators, Apple's iPhones, and AMD's data-center chips — reported third-quarter results this morning that beat on every line. By the closing bell, the stock had fallen 1.8% to $422.15.

## The number behind the number

Two details matter more than the headline. First, the **2-nanometer ramp**: the newest node jumped from 3% of wafer revenue in Q2 to **8%** in Q3, with 3nm at 33% and 5nm at 28% — advanced nodes (7nm and below) now account for **79%** of wafer revenue. Second, **high-performance computing hit 68% of total revenue**, up from 57% a year earlier, while smartphones shrank to 21%. TSMC is no longer a phone-chip company that does AI on the side. It's an AI-chip company.

![2nm ramp: 3% to 8% of wafer revenue in one quarter](https://financialgurkha.com/news/tsmc-highlight-2.jpg)

CEO C.C. Wei's line from the call says it all: AI demand **still exceeds TSMC's supply capability**. The company is working to narrow the gap and is reportedly evaluating expansion beyond Arizona, including Texas.

For Q4, management guided revenue of **$52.3–54.3B** — comfortably above the roughly $48.8B consensus — with gross margins of 65–67%, supported by the steep 2nm ramp.

![Q4 guidance: $52.3–54.3B revenue expected](https://financialgurkha.com/news/tsmc-highlight-3.jpg)

## The closing bell

TSM closed at **$422.15, down 1.8%** from $430.00, on **22.4M shares** — about 70% above average volume. Intraday range: $407.13–$423.66. The stock sits ~13% below its $487.47 all-time high from October 5, with a market cap around $2.2T. For context, the shares are still up ~39% year-to-date.

Wall Street isn't blinking: Needham raised its price target from $530 to $560 today and kept a Buy rating, citing long-term AI growth.

## Our take

This is the foundry pulse of the entire AI trade: when the company that makes everyone else's chips says demand exceeds supply for the sixth straight record quarter, the AI capex cycle is not over — whatever the Nasdaq did today. The selloff looks like classic "priced for perfection" profit-taking on a day the whole tech complex was shaky, not a fundamental crack.

One valuation note: with the 10-year Treasury at ~5.06% and the Fed funds rate at 3.75–4%, the discount rate on future cash flows is doing real work against even great earnings. TSMC's margins (66% gross, 51% net) are the envy of manufacturing — but at these rates, the market demands the growth be not just real, but perpetual. Today's reaction suggests it's starting to ask whether it is.
