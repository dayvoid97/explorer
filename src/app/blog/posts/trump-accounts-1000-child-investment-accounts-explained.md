## 1. What Trump Accounts Are

A Trump Account is a tax-advantaged investment account for a minor child, created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025). In tax law it is an individual retirement account under <Info label="Section 530A">The section of the Internal Revenue Code created in 2025 that defines Trump Accounts — a new variant of traditional IRA whose beneficiary is a minor child.</Info> — a new variant of <Info label="Traditional IRA">A retirement account funded with pretax dollars: contributions may be deductible, growth compounds tax-deferred, and withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.</Info> whose beneficiary is the child. A parent or guardian manages it during childhood; the money belongs to the child.

The mechanics: contributions go in, are invested in U.S. equity index funds, and compound under a lock-up until adulthood. On September 30, 2026, Treasury and the IRS issued temporary regulations ([T.D. 10056](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-tax-report/irs-issues-temporary-regulations-for-trump-accounts-automatic-enrollment)) enabling automatic enrollment; roughly **70 million accounts** were created as of early October 2026.

## 2. The Rules, Briefly

- **Eligibility:** any child under 18 with a valid Social Security number. No income limits, no earned-income requirement.
- **The $1,000 seed:** a one-time Treasury contribution for U.S. citizens with SSNs **born January 1, 2025 – December 31, 2028**. A separate **$250 grant** (funded by a $6.25 billion Dell family pledge) targets children born 2016–2024 in certain ZIP codes.
- **Accessing it:** auto-created accounts are inactive until a parent/guardian **claims** them via the official app at trumpaccounts.gov. Claiming unlocks the seed and enables all contributions. About 10 million of 70 million accounts were funded as of early October.
- **Contributions:** up to **$5,000 per child per year** (inflation-indexed after 2027). Parents may contribute up to $2,500/yr in <Info label="Pretax income">Earnings contributed before income tax is applied — the contribution reduces taxable income in the year it is made, and the money is taxed when withdrawn later.</Info>; employers up to $2,500/yr through a qualifying program (rules not yet final); relatives, friends, local governments, and charities may also contribute — government and charitable money **does not count** toward the $5,000 cap. One account per child.
- **Investments:** qualifying **U.S. equity <Info label="Index funds">Mutual funds or ETFs that track a stock market index (such as the S&P 500) rather than picking individual stocks — the cheapest way to own the whole market.</Info>/ETFs only**, fees capped at **0.10%**. No individual stocks during the growth period.
- **Lock-up:** the <Info label="Growth period">The special-rules phase of a Trump Account, running through December 31 of the year before the child turns 18. During it, contributions are capped, investments are restricted to index funds, and withdrawals are generally prohibited.</Info> runs through December 31 of the year before the child turns 18; withdrawals are generally prohibited. Afterward, **ordinary traditional IRA rules apply** — withdrawals taxed as income.

## 3. Cost-Benefit Analysis: What the Numbers Say

![How $1,000 compounds over 18 years](/blog/trump-accounts/compounding.jpg)

The program's value depends almost entirely on two variables: how much goes in, and what equities return. The fee drag is negligible by design (0.10%), so the analysis is clean.

**Scenario A — the seed only.** $1,000 invested at birth:

| Annual return | Value at age 18 |
| ------------- | --------------- |
| 7% nominal    | ~$3,380         |
| 10% nominal   | ~$5,560         |

The widely cited $200,000-by-age-55 projection assumes decades of uninterrupted high returns, no inflation adjustment, and no taxes. Independent analysis (American Enterprise Institute) adjusting for ~2.4% inflation and taxes at withdrawal puts the realistic purchasing power closer to **~$46,000 in today's dollars** — meaningful, but roughly a quarter of the headline figure. There is no guaranteed return; this is equity market exposure.

**Scenario B — the seed plus maximum family funding.** $1,000 at birth plus $5,000/year for 18 years:

| Annual return | Value at age 18 | Total contributed |
| ------------- | --------------- | ----------------- |
| 7% nominal    | ~$173,000       | ~$91,000          |
| 10% nominal   | ~$234,000       | ~$91,000          |

**Scenario C — the realistic middle.** Most families will contribute irregularly or not at all beyond the seed. The honest expected outcome for the median account is Scenario A plus whatever families add — which is precisely why the claim step matters more than any projection: an unclaimed account compounds at zero.

**Costs to the family:** $0 to claim and open. The real "costs" are opportunity cost (money locked 18 years cannot serve nearer-term needs) and the tax treatment at the exit (traditional-IRA taxation on withdrawal, unlike a Roth or 529's qualified tax-free treatment for education).

**Bottom line of the math:** as a compounding vehicle the structure is sound — low fees, long horizon, equity exposure. As a wealth-building program, its payoff is dominated by private contributions, which is where the distributional questions begin.

## 4. SWOT Analysis

![Weighing the strengths against the weaknesses](/blog/trump-accounts/balance.jpg)

**Strengths**
- Universal seed within the birth window — $1,000 of free equity exposure at birth, no income test.
- Tax-deferred compounding for up to 18 years with a 0.10% fee cap — better pricing than most 401(k)s.
- No earned-income requirement, unlike custodial Roth IRAs.
- The lock-up enforces discipline; the money cannot be raided.
- Employer ($2,500/yr) and philanthropic channels multiply the seed.

**Weaknesses**
- 18-year lock-up: useless for near-term needs; inflexible by design.
- Index-funds-only constraint during the growth period.
- Converts to a traditional IRA at 18 — withdrawals taxed as ordinary income. Families expecting tax-free treatment (as with qualified 529 or Roth withdrawals) will be disappointed.
- Claim friction: ~10 million funded of 70 million created. The last mile is the program's weakest link.
- Temporary regulations expire September 2029; the long-run rulebook is not final.

**Opportunities**
- Compounding from birth is the most powerful and least used lever in household finance; this puts it in reach of families with no brokerage access.
- ~72 companies have committed to matching contributions — the employer channel could become the program's real engine.
- State and philanthropic top-ups (the Dell $6.25B model) can extend the seed beyond the federal birth window.
- A generation that grows up watching an index fund compound may save differently as adults.

**Threats**
- Market risk: no guaranteed return; a child born into a poor 18-year equity stretch gets far less than projections suggest.
- Policy risk: future legislation could alter contribution rules, tax treatment, or the program itself.
- Inflation: any nominal projection must be deflated before it means anything — the $200k headline is the cautionary example.
- Regressive capture: the seed is egalitarian, but Scenario B vs. Scenario A shows the compounding accrues overwhelmingly to families able to fund $5,000/year. The program's total benefit distribution will mirror the wealth distribution unless donor/employer money offsets it.

## 5. Who Should Do What

- **Child born 2025–2028 with an SSN:** claim the account. The $1,000 seed is the highest value-per-effort action in the program and it costs nothing.
- **Any child under 18 with an SSN:** the account itself is free to open; fund it if you can, don't stretch if you can't.
- **Choosing between this and a 529:** for education-specific saving, compare carefully — 529s offer tax-free qualified withdrawals for education; Trump Accounts offer a longer horizon and lower fees but taxable withdrawals. They are complements, not substitutes.
- **Employers:** the $2,500 contribution channel is the program's biggest underused lever, pending final rules.

Evaluate the scheme on its mechanics — free seed, low fees, long lock-up, taxable exit — not on its marketing. On those terms it is a sound compounding vehicle with a narrow but real egalitarian core (the seed) wrapped in a structure whose full benefits scale with private wealth.
