## 1. The Result

Former Sen. Sherrod Brown defeated Sen. Jon Husted in the special election for the rest of the term JD Vance left when he became vice president; Husted had been appointed to the seat in January 2025. With the count still incomplete, Brown had **2,301,042** votes (**50.01%**) to Husted's **2,077,748** (**45.15%**), a gap of about five points (Fox 8). County boards still have to add provisional ballots before certification.

Every major forecaster rated the race a toss-up going into the final days, and several October polls had Brown ahead. Republicans spent more than **$200 million** on advertising to defend the seat (Axios).

## 2. Why Data Centers

Ohio is one of the country's fastest-growing data-center markets, and the power those campuses draw has become a household-bill issue. Brown made it the center of his campaign, calling Husted "the face of data centers" in Ohio (Axios, Ohio Capital Journal).

The message connected two things voters already cared about:

- **Electricity prices.** In the NBC News exit poll, **86%** of voters nationally were concerned about utility costs.
- **Who pays for AI's power.** Data-center growth has pushed up demand forecasts and grid investment, and voters were asked who should carry that cost.

Ohio was not alone. Democratic candidates for governor in Texas and Georgia campaigned on temporary bans on new data-center construction, and Texas elected its candidate (see [How Texas Flipped](/blog/texas-flips-2026-talarico-hinojosa-latino-voters-prices)).

## 3. The Market Read

On election night, Nasdaq-100 futures fell as much as **2.4%** as Senate results came in, led by technology; JPMorgan analysts had flagged the risk of an AI sell-off on expectations of tighter regulation. By 3:13 a.m. Eastern, futures were higher, and strategists described divided government as a check on sweeping new rules (Reuters, via our [election-night report](/blog/democrats-win-congress-2026-midterms)).

The Ohio result is part of why the first reaction happened: a winning Senate campaign built on data centers is a signal about the oversight hearings to come.

## 4. What to Watch

1. **Senate hearings.** A Democratic majority controls committee agendas from January 3, and AI infrastructure is on the list of sector files that reopen.
2. **State utility commissions.** Who pays for new transmission and generation for data centers is decided largely at the state level.
3. **Ohio's certified count.** Provisional ballots will settle the final margin.
