## 1. The week: a rotation, not a crash

Three weeks ago, the 30-year Treasury yield hit a 2002 high and stocks shrugged. Last week, stocks set records while yields climbed further. This week, the bill came due, but only for one part of the market.

| Index | Friday close (Oct 16) | Week | Year to date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nasdaq Composite** | 26,140.03 | **−4.5%** | +12.5% |
| **Russell 2000** | 2,721.37 | **−3.0%** | +9.6% |
| **S&P 500** | 7,659.44 | **−1.9%** | +11.9% |
| **Dow Jones** | 52,002.41 | **+0.7%** | +8.2% |

![A rotation, not a crash: tech sold off while the Dow rose](/images/fig1_week_rotation.png)

The damage came in **two sessions**:

- **Wednesday, CPI day.** The S&P 500 fell **1.1%** and the Nasdaq **1.9%**, the weakest session since July. AI stocks led the decline as investors priced in higher borrowing costs for AI data-center construction. Bank of America fell after missing profit estimates.
- **Friday.** The Nasdaq fell another **2.1%**. Chip makers extended their pullback as investors questioned how long the AI spending boom can last: Micron fell **5.6%** and Intel **5.8%**.

Between them, Thursday brought a brief recovery. Strong corporate earnings lifted the Dow **1.1%**, and the S&P 500 came back within reach of its record. That interruption matters, and I return to it in Section 6.

> *"The S&P 500 and Nasdaq each posted their worst week since June, while the Dow fell far less sharply."* — Associated Press, October 16, 2026

## 2. The trigger: every major inflation print came in hot

![Every major print came in hot](/images/fig2_inflation_surprises.png)

| September data | Actual | Expected | Prior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CPI, year over year** | **3.7%** | 3.6% | 3.4% |
| **Core CPI, year over year** | **2.6%** | 2.5% | 2.4% |
| **PPI, month over month** | **+1.3%** | +0.5% | +0.4% |
| **Core PPI, month over month** | **+0.7%** | +0.3% | +0.2% |
| **PPI, year over year** | **6.3%** | 5.3% | 5.4% |
| **Retail sales, month over month** | **+0.8%** | +0.3% | +1.3% (revised) |

Three details matter more than the headlines:

**1. Energy is the engine.** PPI energy costs jumped **10.8%** in a single month, driven by the Middle East war. That pushed producer-price inflation to **6.3%**, the highest since December 2022.

**2. Consumers are paying more, not buying more.** Gas station sales rose **6.4%**. Excluding them, retail sales grew just **0.2%**.

> *"The large jump in headline retail sales in September was almost entirely due to rising gasoline prices."* — Michael Pearce, Oxford Economics

**3. Core inflation is low, but rising.** At 2.6%, core CPI is far below the headline. But it has now risen two months in a row, the first sign that energy costs are spreading into everything else.

**The Fed response is now priced in.** Markets expect a **hold at the October 27–28 meeting** (about 84–85% odds) and a **hike in December** (about 77%). Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack warned the Fed may need to act more aggressively if public inflation expectations deteriorate.

## 3. The AI paradox: record demand, strained financing

This week's most revealing contrast was between two companies at opposite ends of the AI supply chain.

![The AI paradox: record demand, strained financing](/images/fig3_ai_paradox.png)

| | 🟢 **TSMC: AI demand** | 🔴 **CoreWeave: AI financing** |
|---|---|---|
| **Business model** | Sells the chips, paid in cash | Borrows to buy chips, rents out computing power |
| **This week** | Record Q3 profit of **US$23.6B**, up **68.7%** | Stock down **22%+** for the week; **−15.5%** on Wednesday alone |
| **Key metric** | High-performance computing = **68%** of revenue | 2031 bond yield briefly **above 16%** |
| **Balance sheet** | Free cash flow **NT$477B**, more than triple a year ago | About **$50 billion** of debt |
| **Credit market view** | Six straight quarters of record profit | Default-insurance cost above **1,000 bp**, a record |
| **The lesson** | AI demand is real and accelerating | Debt-funded AI growth gets harder as rates rise |

Both are "AI companies," but they face completely different risks. TSMC sells picks and shovels and gets paid today. CoreWeave borrows today against revenue it expects tomorrow. When rate hikes return to the table, that bet gets more expensive.

**The credit market moved first.** CoreWeave's bonds are on track for a **fifth straight weekly decline**, and the selling spread to similar firms: Nebius fell **6.4%**, IREN **5.5%** and Applied Digital **4.2%**.

Wall Street is divided:

> *"The market may be treating the higher bond yield as 'the business model has stopped working.'"* — Evercore, which kept its outperform rating

> *"The revenue is just not going to be there to turn a profit."* — Todd Schoenberger, CrossCheck Management

> *"You cannot take CoreWeave as the poster child for AI."* — Tommaso Leodari, Index & Cie

**The broader lesson:** AI demand and AI returns are two separate questions. This week, the market began pricing them separately.

## 4. How oil became an AI problem

The week's events form one chain, running from the Strait of Hormuz to Silicon Valley's cost of capital:

![How oil became an AI problem](/images/fig4_transmission_chain.png)

| Link | Evidence this week |
|---|---|
| **1. Oil** | Brent near **$108**, a five-month high |
| **2. Producer prices** | PPI **+1.3%**, energy **+10.8%** |
| **3. Consumer prices** | CPI **3.7%**, above forecast |
| **4. Fed expectations** | December hike odds about **77%** |
| **5. AI credit** | CoreWeave bond yield briefly **above 16%** |
| **6. AI equity** | Nasdaq **−4.5%**, worst week since June |

**The offset that kept the week contained.** Despite hot inflation, the **10-year Treasury yield fell about 14 basis points**, to roughly **5.12%**, as investors moved out of AI trades and into government bonds. In other words, **bonds hedged stocks**. That is the opposite of the stock–bond selloff I flagged as the main risk last week, and it was the single most important stabilizer of the week.

## 5. Banks: strong trading, cautious bankers

The third-quarter earnings season opened with a split message: booming markets businesses and worried executives.

![Bank earnings: strong trading, cautious bankers](/images/fig5_bank_earnings.png)

| Bank | Result | What leadership said |
|---|---|---|
| **Goldman Sachs** | Net income **$5.29B, +29%**; equities trading **+72%** | Rate volatility and data-center opposition are risks to AI infrastructure projects |
| **Citigroup** | Net income **+40%** year over year | Strength in investment banking, services and wealth |
| **JPMorgan** | Net income **$15.3B** vs ~**$16.3B** expected; buybacks cut to **$664M** | Further rate hikes are a **major risk**; the bank is **tightening lending standards** |
| **Bank of America** | Profit **below expectations**; shares fell | Reported on CPI day as hike bets rose |

JPMorgan's warnings deserve the most attention. Jamie Dimon pointed to **weaker loan covenants** and more **payment-in-kind interest**, where borrowers pay interest with new debt instead of cash. These are classic late-cycle credit signals, and they connect directly to the AI financing story: the companies most exposed to higher rates are the ones that borrowed most to grow.

## 6. The risk lens: a sector episode, not a systemic one

My research measures market stress as **episodes**: connected runs of extreme losses, rather than one bad day at a time.

| Paper | What it measures |
|---|---|
| 📄 [**Conditional Tail Episode Risk (TER/CTER)**](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7502499) | How severe a **connected run** of extreme losses becomes in one market |
| 📄 [**Systemic Tail Episode Risk (STER)**](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7534899) | Whether **several markets** are in their tails at the same time, for how long, and with what cumulative damage |

### The episode view of this week

![Episode view: two sharp days, not a continuous run](/images/fig7_episode_view.png)

The Nasdaq's 4.5% weekly loss came mostly from **two sharp days**, Wednesday (−1.88%) and Friday (−2.14%), separated by Thursday's small gain. In TER terms, Thursday **broke the run**. That matters: a market that gets a day to reset, meet margin calls and rebalance is in a very different position from one that suffers the same losses back to back.

### The systemic view of this week

![Reading the week through Tail Episode Risk](/images/fig6_ter_ster_lens.png)

*(A qualitative reading within the TER/STER framework; realized values were not computed for this week.)*

| Lens | This week | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Sector episode** | AI stocks and AI bonds fell together; CoreWeave bonds down five straight weeks | A **synchronized, persistent** episode inside the AI-infrastructure complex |
| **Market episode** | Two sharp Nasdaq days, interrupted by Thursday's recovery | The **run was broken**, limiting cumulative severity |
| **Systemic breadth** | Treasuries rallied; the Dow rose | **Not met.** Diversification held across asset classes |
| **What flips it** | AI credit stress spreading to broad high-yield credit or bank balance sheets, with a December hike on the table | The path from a sector episode to a systemic one |

### Three weeks, one pattern

![Three weeks, three stress episodes, no systemic break](/images/fig8_three_week_series.png)

| Week | What broke | Episode type |
|---|---|---|
| **Sep 28 – Oct 2** | 30-year yield hit a 2002 high; stocks didn't follow | Single-market episode (Treasuries) |
| **Oct 5 – 9** | Stocks hit records while the 10-year hit 5.365% | Treasury stress persists; markets diverge |
| **Oct 12 – 16** | AI stocks and AI credit fell together; Treasuries rallied | Sector episode; breadth **still not met** |

**The pattern is clear.** Stress keeps appearing, but it keeps **rotating between markets** instead of arriving everywhere at once. That rotation is what has kept this a difficult market rather than a crisis. The moment to worry is when the rotation stops, and credit, equities and Treasuries come under pressure **together**. That is exactly the condition STER is built to measure.

## The bottom line

This week, the AI story split in two.

**Demand is booming.** TSMC's record profit shows companies are still buying AI chips as fast as they can be made.

**Financing is straining.** When inflation runs hot and rate hikes return to the table, debt-funded AI infrastructure is the first place the market looks for weakness.

The good news is that the stress stayed contained. Treasuries rallied, the Dow rose, and the market rotated rather than broke. The question for the weeks ahead is whether that containment holds.

> **AI's real test is no longer whether the demand exists. It is whether the returns can outrun the cost of the capital needed to meet it.**
