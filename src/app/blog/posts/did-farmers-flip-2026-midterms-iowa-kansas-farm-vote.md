## 1. What Happened in Iowa and Kansas

Iowa and Kansas are corn, soybean, wheat and cattle states, and both had been reliably Republican in Senate races for a generation. On November 3:

- **Kansas** elected Adam Hamilton, a Kansas City-area pastor who founded the country's largest United Methodist church, over Sen. Roger Marshall, **50.9% to 46.1%**, with independent David Graham at 3.0% and 98.5% of the expected vote counted (Washington Post). Kansas last sent a Democrat to the Senate in **1932**, the longest drought in the country.
- **Iowa** elected state Rep. Josh Turek over Rep. Ashley Hinson for the seat of retiring Sen. Joni Ernst, with **50.8%** at 92% counted (Iowa Capital Dispatch). Iowa also elected Democrat **Rob Sand** governor with about 55%, the first Democratic governor since 2006.

## 2. Kansas: The Tariff Referendum

The Kansas race drew the clearest line on trade of any Senate contest this year.

| | Adam Hamilton (D) | Roger Marshall (R) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tariffs | End the current tariffs | Defended them |
| Future tariffs | Back the **Trade Review Act**: new tariffs end unless Congress approves within 60 days | Opposed it |
| Prices | Blamed tariffs and foreign wars for higher prices | Backed the tariffs and the Iran war |

_Sources: Axios; RTÉ/AFP._

The farm pressure behind that line was specific. Kansas farmers had been hit by **retaliatory tariffs on soybeans and wheat** and by fuel costs that rose with the war in Iran; cattle ranchers were also angry about a plan to ease beef import tariffs (RTÉ/AFP). Hamilton's yard signs showed up in places like rural Neosho County, which voted **73%** for Trump in 2024. His side also outspent Marshall's on ads, about **$16 million to $6 million**.

Marshall was no stranger to farm country: the National Association of Wheat Growers and the Kansas Livestock Association had endorsed him in earlier races. That is what makes the result notable.

## 3. Iowa: Health Care First

Iowa's Senate race was fought mostly on a different issue. Turek campaigned hardest on health care, attacking Hinson for her vote for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and its Medicaid cuts (NPR, Newsweek). Coverage of the race did not center tariffs or soybean prices, even in the country's top soybean state.

That matters for the farmer question: if Iowa's swing was about health care, it may have been driven as much by rural hospitals and Medicaid as by commodity prices, and by voters in mid-sized cities as much as on farms.

## 4. What the Data Says About Farmers

Here is everything measured so far, and what each piece can and cannot show.

| Evidence | Reading | What it shows |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rural registered voters, Aug 12–24 (KFF/AP, n=2,241) | **54% R / 32% D** | Rural voters leaned Republican going in |
| Small-town and rural voters, 2024 (AP VoteCast) | **63%** Trump | The baseline any swing is measured from |
| Young rural voters, 2026 (Tufts CIRCLE) | **R 49 / D 44**, vs Trump 60 / Harris 38 in 2024 | A big swing in one rural subgroup |
| Farmers who "usually" vote Republican, April (Farm Journal) | **35%** still considering their vote | Real openness among farmers before the vote |
| Farmers who "always" vote Republican, April (Farm Journal) | **15%** still considering | Even core voters were weighing it |

**What it adds up to.** Farm-country unhappiness was real and measured. Rural youth swung sharply. But the one direct measure of farmers is a spring poll of intentions, not votes, and the rural crosstabs for 2026 are not yet published. Pre-election reporting on Republican strategy described the party's bigger worry as rural voters staying home, not switching sides (Washington Examiner). A lower Republican rural turnout and a higher Democratic town and suburban turnout could produce the same statewide result as farmers switching.

## 5. What Would Prove It

Three data releases will settle the question:

1. **Certified county results.** If heavily agricultural counties narrowed their Republican margins by more than towns and suburbs did, that points to farm voters moving.
2. **AP VoteCast rural crosstabs.** The 2026 rural and small-town vote share, against the **63%** Trump baseline from 2024.
3. **Turnout by county.** Separates switching from staying home.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Did farmers vote Democratic in the 2026 midterms?**
Not proven yet. Democrats won Senate races in Iowa and Kansas, but no 2026 data isolates farmers, and rural voters leaned Republican 54% to 32% in an August KFF/AP survey.

**Who won the Kansas Senate race in 2026?**
Democrat Adam Hamilton defeated Sen. Roger Marshall, 50.9% to 46.1% with 98.5% of the vote counted. He is the first Democratic senator from Kansas since 1932.

**What is the Trade Review Act?**
A proposal backed by Kansas Senator-elect Adam Hamilton under which new tariffs would end unless Congress approves them within 60 days.

**Who won the Iowa Senate race in 2026?**
Democrat Josh Turek defeated Rep. Ashley Hinson for the seat of retiring Sen. Joni Ernst, with 50.8% of the vote at 92% counted.

**Did Kentucky flip in 2026?**
No. Republican Andy Barr won Kentucky's open Senate seat.

## Conclusion and Key Takeaways

1. **Farm country moved enough to flip two Senate seats**, including Kansas's for the first time since 1932.
2. **Kansas was about tariffs; Iowa was about health care.** "The farm vote" was not one issue.
3. **Farmers switching is a hypothesis, not a finding.** Rural turnout and town-and-suburb swings could explain the same result.
4. **The data to settle it is weeks away**: certified county results, AP VoteCast crosstabs and county turnout.
