> **Key takeaways**
>
> **The class of 2020.** Thirteen investors — Meta, Google, Silver Lake, Vista, KKR, Saudi Arabia's PIF, Mubadala, General Atlantic, ADIA, TPG, L Catterton, Intel Capital and Qualcomm Ventures — invested **₹1,52,056 crore** in Jio Platforms between April and July 2020, for a combined **32.97%**.
>
> **Nobody sold.** The October 2026 IPO was a 100% fresh issue of 27 crore new shares. There was **no offer for sale** — every 2020 investor still holds their stake.
>
> **The paper gain.** Those stakes were worth about **₹3.29 lakh crore at the ₹1,119 issue price**, and about **₹4.17 lakh crore at the October 28 listing close of ₹1,417.50** — roughly **2.7× the money in six years**, unrealised.
>
> **The anchor matters.** Meta and Google bought at a ₹4.36 lakh crore equity value and are up about **2.9×** at the close. The financial investors who paid the stepped-up ₹4.91 lakh crore anchor are up about **2.6×**.

## Thirteen weeks in lockdown

The Jio Platforms fundraising ran from 22 April to 15 July 2020 — the first months of the pandemic, when global markets were supposed to be closed for risk. Instead, Reliance Industries ran the largest private fundraising in Indian corporate history out of a locked-down Mumbai.

It started with the strategic prize. On 22 April 2020, Facebook announced **₹43,573.62 crore** (about $5.7 billion) for **9.99%** of Jio Platforms, struck at a **₹4.62 lakh crore pre-money enterprise value** — an equity value of about ₹4.36 lakh crore. Then the private-equity queue formed. Silver Lake came in twice. Vista, KKR and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund each wrote identical ₹11,367 crore cheques. Mubadala and ADIA brought sovereign money from Abu Dhabi. Intel Capital and Qualcomm Ventures closed the strategic end of the register, and on 15 July Google finished the series with **₹33,737 crore for 7.73%** — expressly priced at the original ₹4.36 lakh crore equity anchor, not the higher one the financial investors had paid.

Reliance's own summary, filed with the exchanges on 30 July 2020, puts the total at **₹152,056 crore across thirteen investors** — a 32.97% stake in what was then an unlisted telecom-and-platforms bet.

## Two anchors, not one

The detail most summaries miss: there were two entry prices.

- **₹4.36 lakh crore equity value** — the Facebook anchor of 22 April 2020. Google, closing the series three months later, was explicitly priced at this same anchor.
- **₹4.91 lakh crore equity value** (₹5.16 lakh crore enterprise value) — the stepped-up price Silver Lake's first tranche set on 4 May 2020, at a stated 12.5% premium to Facebook's equity valuation. Every financial investor after that paid it.

That 12.5% step is why the class of 2020 does not all show the same return today.

## What the stakes are worth now

The IPO priced Jio Platforms at a market value of about **₹10.3 lakh crore** (₹1,119 a share). The stock opened at ₹1,358 on 28 October and closed its first session at **₹1,417.50** on NSE — a market value of about **₹13.0 lakh crore**. The issue was a pure fresh issue, so pre-IPO stakes were diluted only slightly by the 27 crore new shares (a factor of about 0.971).

## Paper gains, deliberately

Three qualifications keep this honest.

**First, none of it is realised.** These are mark-to-market values on stakes the holders chose not to sell into the largest IPO in Indian history. The fresh-issue-only structure was itself a signal: earlier discussions of a sell-down component were dropped, and the register stayed intact.

**Second, the multiple is good, not spectacular — by design.** Two-point-seven times in six years is roughly an 18% compound annual growth rate on the blended entry — strong for cheques of this size, and earned against a business whose return on capital our valuation desk has separately flagged as still catching up with its price (Jio earned a 9.4% ROE in FY26 against the ~24% the IPO price implies). The 2020 investors' cushion is their entry price, not immunity from the same arithmetic.

**Third, control never moved.** Reliance Industries held 66.43% before the IPO and remains the controlling shareholder after it. The class of 2020 owns economics and board visibility, not the steering wheel — the same trade public shareholders are now being offered at ₹1,417.50.

## The bottom line

In thirteen weeks of lockdown, thirteen investors paid ₹1,52,056 crore for a third of Jio Platforms at a ₹4.36–4.91 lakh crore valuation. Six years later the market values the whole company at about ₹13 lakh crore, and not one of them used the biggest exit door India has ever built.

Whether that patience keeps paying depends on the question our [Jio IPO valuation breakdown](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/jio-ipo-valuation-2026-reliance-jio-platforms-quant-analysis) asked of the ₹1,119 price — and which applies with more force at ₹1,417.50: can return on capital climb to meet the price? The class of 2020 can afford to wait for the answer. Anyone buying at the listing close has less room for it to be "not yet."

> **Oversubscription measures demand, not value — but an unsold register measures conviction. The class of 2020 just showed theirs.**
