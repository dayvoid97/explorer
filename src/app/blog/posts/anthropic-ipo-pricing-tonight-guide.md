## The allotment math

The offering is 825 million Class A shares, each carrying one vote. The split matters more than the headline:

- **801,351,354 shares are new**, sold by the company itself. At the $70 to $78 range, that is where the gross proceeds of $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion come from.
- **23,648,646 shares come from selling stockholders** — reported as the seven co-founders. Money from those shares goes to the sellers, not to Anthropic.
- **123,750,000 more shares sit in the underwriters' option** — the greenshoe, a further 15 percent the banks can buy at the offer price within 30 days if demand holds. If it is fully exercised, the deal grows past $70 billion.
- **A separate $26 billion private placement closes alongside**, at the same IPO price, in non-voting Class N shares: Google $10 billion, Nvidia $10 billion, AMD $2.5 billion, and $1 billion each from Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft, with TSMC at $0.5 billion. These are restricted securities and carry no lock-up.
- **Directors and affiliated entities indicated interest in up to $303 million** of stock at the offer price — an indication, not a guarantee of allocation.
- **The syndicate:** Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a group of 22 banks, with JPMorgan running US retail distribution. The November 2 amendment named the retail brokers through which public orders were taken: Robinhood, Fidelity, Charles Schwab, SoFi and E*TRADE.

At $78, the top of the range, the price values Anthropic at about $1 trillion on shares outstanding, and up to about $1.405 trillion fully diluted by CNBC's calculation. The last private mark, the May 2026 Series H, valued the company at about $965 billion — so even the bottom of the range prices in a premium to the spring round. The filing puts run-rate revenue at about $81 billion as of October 31, with third-quarter revenue of about $17 billion in the November amendment. We broke the filing down in full in [Inside Anthropic's S-1](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/anthropic-s1-ipo-filing-complete-analysis) and charted the growth in [Anthropic Revenue Growth: $41 Million to $11.6 Billion a Quarter](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/anthropic-revenue-growth-2023-2026-quarterly-annual).

## The demand picture

By the accounts of the banks' own coverage this week, the book is heavily oversubscribed: Bloomberg reported orders at nearly seven times the shares available, and five existing backers, Sequoia among them, plan to buy billions of dollars of stock in the offering itself. Gray-market perpetual contracts changed hands near $104 a share on Monday, a third above the top of the range — treat that as a sentiment gauge from a thin, speculative market, not a forecast. The honest summary is that demand is not the question tonight. The price the underwriters choose, and what the open market does with it tomorrow, are the questions.

## The lock-up: the deadlines that run into 2027

About 15.5 billion shares — the stock held by founders, employees and early investors — sit under lock-up or market-standoff agreements. Unlike the standard single cliff, Anthropic's release is staggered by holder group, counted from the final prospectus date (today, November 18):

- **From the first trading day:** up to about 316 million shares, mainly a management and employee tranche, can become eligible in stages.
- **Before day 180:** about 3.6 billion of the locked shares may become eligible through the staged windows.
- **Around day 180 — mid-May 2027:** the main release for most holders, which can arrive earlier, on the second full trading day after Anthropic reports Q1 2027 results.
- **Up to day 365 — November 2027:** the outer limit for the co-founders and for holders of more than 0.5 percent of the fully diluted shares. At the first window, founders can sell up to 29 percent of their locked shares.
- The underwriters can waive restrictions early, in whole or in part. None has been reported.

Each window is a supply event: shares that could not be sold become shares that can. That calendar, more than any single headline, is the schedule a holder of ANT will live by in 2027. The control structure those shares sit inside is its own story — we covered it in [Who Controls Anthropic](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/who-controls-anthropic-share-classes-long-term-benefit-trust).

## The Figma parallel

Sixteen months ago, the market ran this exact experiment at a smaller scale, and it was genuinely spectacular — one of the great IPO debuts of the modern era. We have covered the company since in our [intrinsic valuation of Figma](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/figma-intrinsic-valuation). The record, from Bloomberg's data as carried in company profiles and from Jay Ritter's IPO dataset at the University of Florida:

- Figma priced on July 30, 2025 at **$33** a share — the top of a raised range — selling **36,937,079 shares** (about 12.5 million new from the company, about 24.5 million from selling holders) and raising **$1.22 billion**. The deal was about **40 times oversubscribed**, and only about **7 to 9 percent** of the company was floated.
- Trading began on the New York Stock Exchange on **July 31, 2025**. The first trade was **$85**, up 158 percent before a single public investor had a chance to think. It **closed the first day at $115.50, up 250 percent** — the largest first-day pop in more than three decades for a US IPO raising over $1 billion. It closed day two higher still, at $122, and printed an intraday peak of **$142.92**, a market value of about $68 billion.
- The returns were real, and so was the bill. Ritter's dataset puts the money left on the table — the gap between the $33 offer price and the $115.50 first close, multiplied by the shares sold — at **$3,047,309,018**. The Wall Street Journal's headline was "Software IPO Left $3 Billion on Table." That $3 billion is the IPO discount in its purest form: value the company and its selling holders handed to whoever received an allocation, for the price of showing up.
- Then the round trip. By August 4, four sessions in, the stock had fallen a third from its day-two close to $88.60. Figma's lock-up had a performance trigger — 25 percent above the IPO price for five straight days released a quarter of the locked shares at day 36 — and the pop itself tripped it on day one, putting insider supply into a falling market. Executives sold through the autumn. By February 2026 the shares were near $22, and by the middle of 2026 they touched about $16.60. They have recently traded near $21.75, a market value of about $11.5 billion — **down about 85 percent from the peak, and below the $33 IPO price**.

**What each investor actually got.** The same stock produced four different investments, depending on the door you entered through:

- **Allocated at $33 and sold at the first close:** up 250 percent in a day — the impressive return the headlines were about.
- **Allocated at $33 and still holding at about $21.75:** down about 34 percent. Even the best seat in the house is under water sixteen months later.
- **Bought the first close at $115.50:** down about 81 percent.
- **Bought the peak at $142.92:** down about 85 percent. Ten thousand dollars at the top is about $1,520 today.

**The discount rate Figma priced into.** An IPO price is a bundle of future cash flows divided by a discount rate, and Figma's backdrop was forgiving. The day before the debut, July 30, 2025, the Federal Reserve held the federal funds rate at **4.25 to 4.50 percent** — a 9–2 vote, with Governors Bowman and Waller dissenting in favour of a cut. The **10-year Treasury, the risk-free anchor of every valuation model, closed Figma's debut day at 4.37 percent**. Inflation was 2.7 percent (June CPI), unemployment 4.1 percent, the S&P 500 was at record highs and the VIX sat near 16 — with an August 1 tariff deadline as the one visible cloud. On the method we used in our [Anthropic discount-rate analysis](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/anthropic-ipo-risk-factors-discount-rate-valuation), that backdrop supports an illustrative cost of equity around 10 to 11 percent for a growth stock.

Tonight's backdrop is harsher where it matters. The policy rate is lower — 3.75 to 4.00 percent after the Fed's October hold — but the long end, the rate that actually discounts a decade of future cash flows, is higher: the 10-year Treasury printed **5.10 percent on November 17**, against Figma's 4.37 percent. Our October analysis worked with the 10-year at 5.00 percent and an illustrative cost of equity of 11 to 12 percent, and showed that a single point on the discount rate can move a long-duration valuation by about a fifth. Figma got its record pop in a 4.37 percent world. Anthropic is asking for $1.4 trillion fully diluted in a 5.10 percent world. Same theatre, more expensive money.

- And here is the part that makes Figma a lesson rather than a punchline: **the business never broke.** Revenue grew 46 percent in the first quarter of 2026 to $333.4 million, net dollar retention ran at 139 percent, the company crossed $1 billion in annualised revenue, and in the third quarter of 2026 — which we covered on November 4 — revenue grew 43 percent to $391.4 million. What collapsed was not the company. It was the price the opening market had paid for it, repriced as investors weighed whether AI design tools — including, with some irony, Anthropic's own Claude Design, launched in April 2026 — would route around Figma's canvas.

The parallels to tonight are structural, not decorative. A heavily oversubscribed book. A price likely to be set at or above the range. A float that is a small fraction of the company — Anthropic is offering about 5 percent of its fully diluted shares, with the $26 billion placement on top. A staggered lock-up that starts releasing supply almost immediately instead of all at once. And a valuation that already assumes the growth story goes right. Figma says the first print of the stock price is the market's excitement, not its verdict. The verdict arrives over quarters — in reported revenue, in margins, and at every lock-up window.

None of that argues the Anthropic debut will follow Figma's path; the companies differ in scale, market and moment, and Anthropic's $81 billion run-rate is a different animal from Figma's revenue at its debut. It argues something narrower and more useful: on pricing night, the calendar and the allotment sheet tell you more than the pop will.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When does Anthropic price its IPO, and when does ANT start trading?**
Pricing is expected tonight, Wednesday, November 18, 2026, after the close, with the first trade on Thursday, November 19, on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. Those dates come from CNBC's reporting; the prospectus itself fixes no dates.

**How many shares is Anthropic selling, and to whom?**
825 million Class A shares: about 801.4 million new shares from the company and about 23.6 million from selling stockholders, reported as the seven co-founders. The underwriters hold an option on 123.75 million more. Alongside, seven strategic partners buy $26 billion of non-voting Class N shares at the IPO price in a private placement.

**What is the price range and the implied valuation?**
$70 to $78 a share, raising $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion before the greenshoe. At $78, the value is about $1 trillion on shares outstanding and up to about $1.405 trillion fully diluted, by CNBC's calculation. Reporting this week says the final price could land above the range.

**When can Anthropic insiders sell?**
On a staggered schedule. Small tranches can release from the first trading day; about 3.6 billion shares may become eligible before day 180; the main release for most holders lands around day 180 (mid-May 2027) or just after Q1 2027 results, whichever comes first; co-founders and the largest holders face an outer limit of 365 days, in November 2027.

**What happened to Figma after its IPO?**
Figma priced at $33 on July 30, 2025 and began trading the next day: first trade $85, first close $115.50 (up 250 percent), a peak of $142.92 — then a fall of about 85 percent from the peak over the following year, to below its IPO price, even as revenue kept growing at 40 percent or more. Jay Ritter's dataset puts the underpricing at $3.05 billion left on the table. The repricing was about valuation, lock-up supply and AI-disruption risk, not about the business failing.

**What was the discount-rate backdrop for the Figma IPO?**
Forgiving. The Fed held its policy rate at 4.25 to 4.50 percent the day before the debut, and the 10-year Treasury closed debut day at 4.37 percent — against 5.10 percent on November 17, 2026, the eve of Anthropic's pricing. Inflation was 2.7 percent and unemployment 4.1 percent in June 2025.
