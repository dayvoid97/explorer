## 1. North Carolina: A Rout Called at 8:15

North Carolina was expected to be one of the closest and most expensive races of the year. It was neither. The AP called it for Roy Cooper at **8:15 p.m.**, less than ten minutes after polls closed, and Michael Whatley conceded soon after (WRAL). With most of the vote in, Cooper led **58.7% to 39.2%**, beating his final polling average of 52.8–41.8 by about nine points on the margin (RealClearPolling).

**Why it was lopsided:**

- **Name recognition.** Cooper had won six statewide races, four for attorney general and two for governor, and had never lost an election in the state. Many voters did not recognize Whatley's name (Carolina Public Press).
- **Money.** Cooper entered October with **$12.5 million** on hand to Whatley's **$916,000**, a ratio of about 13 to 1.
- **Coalition.** Early ABC News voter polls had Cooper winning about **6 in 10 independents**, college-educated voters by more than 30 points and voters aged 18–29 by about 58 points. Non-college voters split evenly, and about 9 in 10 Black voters backed him.

Cooper takes the seat of retiring Sen. Thom Tillis, the first Democratic Senate win in the state since Kay Hagan in 2008.

## 2. South Carolina: The Experience Question

South Carolina's race began with a death. Sen. Lindsey Graham died suddenly on **July 11**, and his sister, **Darline Graham**, was appointed to finish his term and ran for the seat with the president's endorsement. The president rallied for her twice in the final two weeks.

Annie Andrews, a pediatrician and first-time Senate nominee, won **50.6% to 47.2%** with about 93% of the vote counted, a margin of about **67,760 votes** (PBS).

The AP Voter Poll found the race turned on one question:

| AP Voter Poll finding | Share |
| :--- | :--- |
| Voters who said Darline Graham lacked the right experience | about **2 in 3** |
| Of those voters, the share who voted for Andrews | about **7 in 10** |

_Source: AP Voter Poll, via Hearst Television and TIME._

Andrews pointed to her fundraising as a strength, while Graham's campaign struggled to raise money. She is the first Democrat to win a Senate race in South Carolina since Fritz Hollings's last re-election in **1998**, and the first woman the state has elected to the Senate. The seat itself had been Republican since Strom Thurmond switched parties in 1964.

## 3. What the Two Results Share

- **Candidate quality decided the margin.** In both states, one candidate was far better known or far better qualified in voters' eyes.
- **The national tide set the floor.** About nine in ten voters nationally called the cost of living a crucial or major factor (CNN exit poll), and most said the tariffs hurt the economy (CBS News).
- **South Carolina is the bigger surprise.** North Carolina is a swing state that Cooper had won before; South Carolina had not elected a Democrat to the Senate in 28 years.

## Conclusion and Key Takeaways

1. **North Carolina was decided by the candidates long before election night**: fame and a 13-to-1 cash edge.
2. **South Carolina was decided by experience**, the question two in three voters raised about the appointed senator.
3. **Both are flips, but only one is a realignment signal**, and it is South Carolina's. Watch whether it holds in 2028.
