# The $24 Billion Leak: Moderna's Melanoma Data Is Real — and Melanoma Alone Doesn't Carry $200 a Share

At about 12:22 p.m. Eastern on Thursday, a screenshot appeared on X with four characters that moved a market: "HR is .58."

The screenshot was the embargoed late-breaking abstract for ESMO 2026's Presidential Symposium — the full Phase 3 results for intismeran autogene, Moderna and Merck's personalised mRNA cancer therapy, combined with Keytruda in resected melanoma. The abstract had surfaced, briefly, on the medical news site Oncology Central, two days before Prof. Georgina Long was due to present it in Madrid. It was taken down. Screenshots do not come down.

What followed, in order: Moderna's stock was briefly halted for volatility. At 2:11 p.m., ESMO confirmed on X that the embargo "has been breached," released the abstract and lifted the embargo for everyone. At 2:59 p.m., Merck and Moderna issued the full results themselves. By the close, MRNA had fallen from $245.81 to $202.63 — down $43.18, or 17.6%, on roughly 50 million shares, triple the prior day's volume. Intraday it was far worse: at about $183.88 the stock was down more than 25%, and roughly $24 billion of market value had been wiped out in an afternoon (Reuters). Merck, the senior partner, closed down about 1.8%. By midday Friday, MRNA had clawed back to about $206.

Two clarifications before anything else, because the headlines blurred both. First, the drop at the close was 17.6%, not the "20%" in most headlines — 20%-plus was the intraday print. Second, the leak was Thursday lunchtime, not Wednesday; Wednesday's action was a run-up into the print, to a 52-week high.

And the question underneath the chaos: was the leaked number bad? We verified it against the primary source. It was not bad. It was, in the narrow sense that matters to this article, *smaller than the fantasy* — and the distance between those two things is where $24 billion went.

## The data: the 42% is real, and it is Phase 3

The figure circulating from Yahoo's coverage — "a 42% reduction in recurrence or death risk versus Keytruda alone" — is exact, and it is new. It comes from the prespecified primary analysis of INTerpath-001 (V940-001, NCT05933577), the companies' joint release of October 22, 2026:

| INTerpath-001 (Phase 3) | Intismeran + Keytruda | Placebo + Keytruda |
|---|---|---|
| Patients (resected stage IIB–IV cutaneous melanoma) | 757 | 380 |
| Recurrence-free survival (primary) | **HR 0.58** (95% CI 0.45–0.75), p of less than 0.001 | — |
| 2-year RFS | **82.2%** | 70.0% |
| Distant metastasis-free survival | **HR 0.62** (95% CI 0.45–0.85), p = 0.002 | — |
| 2-year DMFS | **88.5%** | 80.2% |
| Grade 3–5 treatment-related adverse events | 14.2% | 13.4% |

Source: Merck/Moderna press release, October 22, 2026 (Business Wire). Median follow-up 24.6 months; data cutoff June 30, 2026.

Read the primary line plainly: adding a personalised vaccine — built from each patient's own tumour, encoding up to 34 of its neoantigens, nine doses over about a year — to standard Keytruda cut the risk of the cancer coming back, or of death, by 42%. In absolute terms, 82 of every 100 treated patients were recurrence-free at two years against 70 of 100 on Keytruda alone: roughly one recurrence prevented for every eight patients treated. The trial was large (1,137 patients), double-blind and placebo-controlled — the evidentiary standard the Phase 2 data never had. Overall survival is not yet mature; the companies report a favourable trend and nothing more, and the 42% should not be read as a proven survival benefit. The paper has been accepted by the *New England Journal of Medicine*.

Three details inside the release deserve more attention than the headline got:

**The benefit arrives late.** In a prespecified sensitivity analysis, the RFS hazard ratio for the first six months was 0.99 — no measurable benefit at all while patients were still being vaccinated — and 0.42 *after* six months, a 58% risk reduction. That is exactly the signature an immunologist would predict: the therapy has to teach the immune system before it can protect it. It also means the curves separate late, which matters for how the benefit compounds at five years.

**The distant-metastasis number decayed versus Phase 2.** In the small Phase 2b trial (KEYNOTE-942, 157 patients), the five-year update in January 2026 showed a 49% RFS reduction (HR 0.510) and a DMFS hazard ratio of 0.411. Phase 3's DMFS of 0.62 is a real effect — but against the 0.411 anchor in investors' heads, it read as decay, and TD Cowen said so on the record. Some of the gap is arithmetic, not biology: Phase 3 enrolled earlier-stage patients (IIB/IIC as well as III/IV), whose cancers recur less and metastasise less on the control arm too, which mechanically compresses the ratio.

**The comparator problem is real.** RBC and BMO both raised it within hours: simply giving pembrolizumab *before* surgery (the neoadjuvant approach, SWOG S1801) produced a similar hazard ratio against adjuvant pembrolizumab — at a fraction of the cost of manufacturing a bespoke vaccine for every patient. Intismeran's rejoinder is durability and the post-six-month separation, but the question "why not just sequence the cheap drug better?" is now the central commercial debate, not a footnote.

## Why a good trial knocked 25% off the stock intraday

Because of where the stock was standing when the number landed. On August 19, Moderna announced the trial had met its endpoints — with no effect sizes disclosed — and the stock rose 177% in a single session, its largest one-day gain ever, from $62.96 to $174.38. It then ran a further ~40% into Wednesday's $245.81 close: up more than 500% in 2026, into the Nasdaq-100, with a market value of about $97 billion on a business whose trailing revenue is about $2.2 billion.

Against that positioning, HR 0.58 was, in RBC's phrase, at the low end of the hoped-for 0.50–0.59 range — a beat against the formal bar analysts had set (UBS had said HR 0.65 could drive a rally; Leerink called anything under 0.60 transformative), and a miss against the Phase 2 ghost of 0.51. UBS, William Blair and Bernstein all used the same words within hours: strong data, *sell-the-news*. The data did not change on Thursday. The price of perfection did.

## Keytruda: the giant standing next to the vaccine

Every number in this story is downstream of one drug, so it is worth being precise about it.

**What it is.** Keytruda (pembrolizumab) is Merck & Co.'s anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody: it blocks the PD-1 receptor on T cells, releasing the brake that tumours use to switch those cells off. It is, by revenue, the best-selling prescription drug in the world, approved across more than 40 indications, including — on its own — the exact adjuvant melanoma population intismeran now joins it in.

**What it sells.** Merck-reported figures:

| Period | Keytruda (and Qlex family) sales | Share of Merck total sales |
|---|---|---|
| FY 2024 | $29.5B | 45.9% |
| FY 2025 | $31.7B | 48.7% |
| Q1 2026 | $8.03B | ~49% |
| Q2 2026 | $8.37B | ~50% |

Sources: Merck full-year 2024 and 2025 results releases; Merck Q1 2026 release; Reuters, August 4, 2026 (Q2). Consensus (Evaluate, via BioPharma Dive) has the family peaking near $35.4B in 2027 — which is as good a definition of Keytruda's total addressable market as exists: one drug, selling roughly $32–35 billion a year, half of a top-ten pharmaceutical company.

**The cliff.** Keytruda's US compound patent expires in **December 2028**. That single fact explains the entire intismeran programme's strategic value to Merck: every melanoma patient on the combination is a Keytruda patient, and a patented combination is a lifeboat for volume that biosimilars would otherwise take. Merck's subcutaneous version, Keytruda Qlex (approved September 2025, its own patent estate to 2043), is the other lifeboat.

**The split.** Under the 2022 collaboration, Merck and Moderna share the costs and profits of intismeran **equally, worldwide** — Merck paid a $250 million option exercise fee in October 2022. Merck commercialises and books all intismeran sales; Moderna manufactures and books its 50% share of the programme's profit. Keytruda's own revenue stays 100% Merck's. Hold that structure in mind; it decides how much of the melanoma opportunity below actually lands in Moderna's income statement — half of the profit, and none of the headline sales.

Two post-quarter events change this picture and belong in any valuation: in July, Moderna paid a **$950 million litigation settlement** (it sat in that accrued-liabilities line); on September 1, it completed **$3.0 billion of 0% convertible notes due 2032** (initial conversion price $210.58) — raised, opportunistically, into the August re-rating. Pro forma for both, cash and investments were roughly $9.0 billion before the second half's operating burn, against roughly $3.6 billion of debt including the converts.

**Cash flow.** The direction is the story: operating cash flow was **+$13.6 billion in 2021** and +$5.0 billion in 2022 (FY2022 release); by FY2025 it was **−$1.87 billion**, with free cash flow of −$2.07 billion. In the first half of 2026 the cash and investments balance fell from $8.1 billion to $6.9 billion. This is a company that has been funding a roughly $3 billion-a-year loss engine out of a pile earned in two extraordinary years — which brings us to those years.

## The COVID money: what "printing money" actually looked like

It is easy to forget the scale, so here it is in one paragraph of filings. In 2020, Moderna's revenue was $803 million. In 2021 it was **$18.5 billion**, and in 2022 **$19.3 billion** — $37.7 billion in two years, of which COVID-19 vaccine product sales were about $36 billion, sold to governments on advance purchase agreements at pandemic prices with pandemic gross margins (85% in 2021). Net income across the two years: **$20.6 billion**. Operating cash flow: $18.6 billion. By December 31, 2022, the balance sheet held **$18.2 billion** in cash and investments. There was no debt. For two years, Moderna was not a biotech company with a vaccine; it was one of the most profitable enterprises on earth per employee, because the world was buying its single product at emergency scale.

Where did it go? Into three buckets, all visible in the filings: **$3.3 billion of share buybacks in 2022 alone** (27 million shares retired between late 2021 and end-2022, at prices far above where the stock traded in 2025); a manufacturing and R&D footprint built for a much larger company (R&D peaked at $4.8 billion in 2023); and the losses of the hangover — **−$4.7B, −$3.6B and −$2.8B across 2023, 2024 and 2025** as vaccine revenue fell 90% from peak while the cost base came down slowly. Cumulative damage since the peak: revenue down from $19.3B to $1.9B, cash and investments down from $18.2B to $6.9B, retained earnings down to $5.1B.

That history is not trivia; it is the valuation's starting condition. Moderna today is a roughly $2 billion-revenue, loss-making respiratory-vaccine business attached to (a) about $9 billion of pro-forma cash, (b) a pipeline, and (c) one Phase 3-validated oncology asset. The market is being asked to price (b) and (c). So let us price (c) properly.

## The valuation: melanoma as a real option

A positive Phase 3 asset that has not yet been filed is not an annuity; it is an option — on regulatory approval, on label breadth, on commercial execution against a cheaper comparator, with a floor near zero and a long right tail. The honest way to value it is the way the industry does: build the cash flows as if it works, then multiply by the probability that it works (a risk-adjusted NPV — the financial form of a real-options value), and show every assumption so you can change the ones you dislike.

**Assumptions (base case)**

| Assumption | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | 2027 | Companies engaging regulators; CEO guided to a US filing and expected 2027 launch (Reuters) |
| Partnership sales, melanoma only | Ramp $0.15B (2027) → peak **$3.0B** (2033–34) → $1.2B by 2040 | Barclays: about $3B melanoma sales by 2035; FactSet consensus peak about $2.0B (2030) is the conservative bound |
| Partnership operating margin | 20% at launch → 50% at peak | Personalised manufacturing is COGS-heavy early; pembrolizumab economics are Merck's own |
| Moderna's share of profit | **50%** | The 2022 collaboration, verified |
| Probability of approval | **85%** | Positive prespecified Phase 3 primary analysis in hand; pre-filing |
| Tax | 0% | Net operating losses and a full valuation allowance (Q2 10-Q) shelter early profits |
| Discount rate | **5%** | The 10-year US Treasury, as specified for this analysis; sensitivities below |
| Shares | 396M basic | 10-Q cover, June 30, 2026 (the converts add ~14M shares if converted at $210.58) |

**Result — what the melanoma option is worth to a Moderna shareholder**

| Case (melanoma peak sales) | rNPV @ 5% | Per share |
|---|---|---|
| Bear — $1.5B peak, 75% approval odds | $1.7B | **$4.31** |
| **Base — $3.0B peak, 85% approval odds** | **$3.9B** | **$9.84** |
| Bull — $5.0B peak, 90% approval odds | $7.1B | **$17.92** |

Strip the probability haircut out of the base case — assume approval and the ramp both happen — and the stream is worth $4.6 billion, or $11.57 a share. Discount the base case like a banker instead of a Treasury and it compresses: $7.92 a share at 8%, $6.90 at 10%, $6.05 at 12%. Melanoma, on any of these, is worth roughly **$4 to $18 a share** — real money, a genuine second act for the income statement (peak profit share of about $750 million a year to Moderna in the base case, against a current roughly $3 billion annual loss) — and, on its own, nowhere near $200.

Apply the arithmetic in its simplest form: Thursday's close of $202.63, plus the base-case melanoma option of $9.84, is **$212.47**. That is what the share would be worth if today's price contained *no* melanoma value at all and you credited the programme on top. It plainly does contain melanoma value — August 19's +177% was nothing else — so the correct exercise is the reverse: take the price apart and see what else must be true.

**What is left: the core business on a DCF**

Value the rest of Moderna — the respiratory franchise (COVID, the newly approved flu vaccine mFLUSIVA, the EU-approved flu/COVID combination), the cost base management is cutting, and the balance sheet — on a discounted cash flow at the same 5%. Our path: free cash flow of −$1.6B in 2027 narrowing to breakeven around 2029 as flu revenue builds and opex falls, reaching about +$1.3B by 2035, a zero-growth terminal value on that figure, plus pro-forma net cash of about $5.4 billion (cash after the settlement and the converts, less the debt including those converts).

| Discount rate | Core equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|
| **5% (10-year Treasury)** | $23.3B | **$58.95** |
| 8% | $14.0B | $35.40 |
| 10% | $11.0B | $27.84 |
| 12% | $9.1B | $22.97 |

Be as suspicious of the 5% column as we are: at a Treasury discount rate, $16.8 billion of the core's $23.3 billion is terminal value — the mathematics of a 5% world makes distant cash flows enormous, which is precisely why the market does not actually discount Moderna at 5%. At a conventional 10–12% biotech cost of capital, the core is worth roughly **$23–28 a share**.

**The decomposition — what $202.63 is actually made of (at the specified 5%)**

| Component | Per share |
|---|---|
| Core business (DCF, incl. net cash) | $58.95 |
| Melanoma option (base case rNPV) | $9.84 |
| **Residual: everything else the market is paying for** | **$133.84** |
| **Thursday's close** | **$202.63** |

The residual is about **$53 billion** for the options that have not read out yet: three Phase 3 trials of intismeran in non-small cell lung cancer, the kidney-cancer study (readout expected late 2026 or early 2027), bladder, a Phase 1 pancreatic programme, and the platform claim underneath all of them — that a therapy designed from a patient's tumour in weeks can be pointed at any cancer. The unadjusted anchors for that stack, from the banks, show how wide the possibility cone is: Wolfe Research models $9.2 billion of peak revenue across four indications; Leerink's partnership bull case is $10 billion; Jefferies, unadjusted and at a $260,000 price, models $54 billion globally. TD Cowen — which raised its peak intismeran *profit* estimate to about $3.4 billion by 2035 and its target from $40 to $90 on October 21 — is effectively saying the residual should be a fraction of $53 billion. The market, at $202.63 and even more at Wednesday's $245.81, is saying the platform is probably real.

## What happens next

The calendar is unusually dense, and it is dated. **Saturday, October 24:** the full INTerpath-001 presentation at ESMO in Madrid (LBA1, Presidential Symposium) plus Moderna's investor event the same evening — watch the subgroup detail: benefit by stage, by tumour mutational burden, and any overall-survival colour. **November 6:** Q3 results — the first balance sheet with the settlement paid and the converts in. **Late 2026 / early 2027:** the kidney-cancer readout, the next option to resolve. **November 12:** Analyst Day. And through 2027, the filing and launch path for melanoma itself.

## Bottom line

The claim checks out: 42%, Phase 3, 1,137 patients, p of less than 0.001, from the primary source. The leak changed none of the data; it changed who got to trade on it first, and it exposed how much of Moderna's price was expectation rather than asset. Melanoma — valued honestly, as an option, at the Treasury's own discount rate — is worth about $10 a share in the base case and under $18 in the bull case. The core business is worth $59 a share at that same generous rate, and $23–28 at rates investors actually use. Which means the $200 question was never really about melanoma. It is about whether a platform that has now produced one positive Phase 3 is worth $53 billion of options on the next four. Thursday's sellers said less of it is proven than Wednesday's buyers thought. Both were pricing the same trial. Only one of them had read it — and now, embargo or no embargo, everyone has.
