## The setup

The thesis, stated plainly: Rent the Runway is growing revenue at 20%+, its net loss just halved, and a 2025 recapitalization cut its debt roughly in half. The stock trades at $1.72 — down 78% this year, hugging its 52-week low. Growing business, shrinking losses, cleaned-up balance sheet, all-time-low price. The kind of setup that makes you reach for a spreadsheet.

So I did. And the spreadsheet has opinions — some of them uncomfortable.

## Revenue and the turnaround story

The operating turnaround is real. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended July 31, 2026, reported September 11) was the company's best quarter on record by revenue:

![Q2 FY2026 vs Q2 FY2025: revenue record, losses halved](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/rent-q2-yoy.jpg)

| Metric | Q2 FY2025 | Q2 FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $80.9M | **$97.7M** | +20.8% (all-time quarterly high) |
| Gross margin | 30.0% | **36.1%** | +609 bps |
| Net loss | $(26.4)M | **$(12.9)M** | loss halved |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $3.6M (4.4%) | **$12.6M (12.9%)** | +3.5x |
| Ending active subscribers | 146,373 | 140,826 | −3.8% |
| Cash | $43.6M | $29.0M | — |

Two things to note. First, subscriber count actually slipped 3.8% — the growth is coming from monetization, not headcount: add-on bookings were up 81% year-over-year, and a third of subscribers now use add-ons. Second, Q1 was even hotter (+29.2% to $89.9M). Full-year FY2025 revenue was $329.8M (+7.7%), and management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of double-digit revenue growth with 4–7% adjusted EBITDA margins.

The business is genuinely improving. Now the part the stock price is actually about.

## The debt: halved, but at a price

The October 2025 recapitalization did exactly what the thesis says — it cleared a lot of debt:

![Long-term debt cut in half by the October 2025 recap](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/rent-debt.jpg)

Long-term debt fell from **$333.7M** (January 2025) to **$156.6M** (January 2026), sitting at $157.5M as of July 2026. The mechanics: the lender exchanged $100M of debt for new term loans, contributed the rest above $100M in exchange for equity, and put in $20M of fresh loans. Remaining principal: $120M, maturing October 2029, at SOFR + 500 bps. Then, on September 1, 2026, the company drew another $10M incremental term loan for working capital — the debt is creeping back up.

But "cleared debt" is the wrong verb. The right one is **converted** — converted into equity. And that brings us to the dilution.

## The dilution: this is the whole story

In October 2025, lenders received **26,175,193 newly issued shares** in exchange for their debt — taking roughly 86% of the post-recap equity. Existing shareholders were left with about 14% of a company they'd previously owned nearly all of.

![Shares outstanding: the recap diluted holders roughly 9x](https://financialgurkha.com/blog/rent-dilution.jpg)

The longer history is worse. There was a 1-for-20 reverse split in April 2024 to stay Nasdaq-compliant. Weighted-average shares went from 3.59M (FY2023) to 11.96M (FY2025) to ~33.4M outstanding after the recap. And it's not over: a **$15M rights offering at $3.55/share** (up to ~4.2M shares, ~12.6% further dilution) expired October 14, 2026 — fully backstopped by the lender group, meaning any shares retail didn't buy went straight to the lenders, increasing their grip.

So: does the company have a history of diluting shareholders? The filings answer that one themselves. The lenders now own the company; public shareholders own a stub.

Two more balance-sheet facts for the file: stockholders' equity is **negative $65.5M** (the company is balance-sheet insolvent on a book basis), and **no public credit rating exists** — Moody's and S&P do not rate this debt; it's private credit. Don't let anyone tell you it's "rated CCC" — it's unrated.

## Short interest

As of September 30, 2026: **1.64M shares short, 6.79% of float, 13.26 days to cover.** That's moderately elevated — and the days-to-cover figure is striking, but it reflects thin trading volume rather than a massive absolute short. Context: short interest spiked to ~32% of float in November 2025 during the recap chaos, then collapsed as the float expanded ~9x. The current 6.8% is a far cry from a crowded short — the price action is about the recap overhang and the 28.5M shares registered for resale by the selling stockholders, not a short squeeze setup.

## The valuation

Same discipline as our Figma piece: state the inputs, show the arithmetic, let the reader judge.

**Anchors (all from filings):** FY2025 revenue $329.8M. FY2026E ~$369M (double-digit guidance; H1 already at $187.6M). Net debt $128.5M ($157.5M debt − $29.0M cash; the $10M September loan adds ~$10M on top). ~33.4M shares outstanding, ~35M fully diluted with the rights offering.

**Discount rate:** the 10-year Treasury sits at ~5.06% today. Cost of equity ≈ 5.06% + 4.5% equity risk premium × 1.6 beta ≈ 12.3%. Cost of debt ≈ 9.4% (SOFR + 500 bps, no tax shield — the company has NOLs). Blended discount rate: **11%**.

**Base case** — revenue growth 10%→6% over four years, adjusted EBITDA margins rising from the guided 5.5% to 12.5%, rental-product capex normalizing from 13% to 10.5% of revenue, 2.5% terminal growth:

- Implied enterprise value: **−$33M**
- Implied equity value: **−$161M → ~$0/share**

Read that again. Under assumptions that are arguably generous (margins more than doubling, capex discipline the company has never shown), the enterprise is worth less than its net debt. The base case says the equity is an out-of-the-money option — which, at $1.72, is roughly what the market is treating it as.

**Reverse check** — what does $1.72 actually imply? At $61.8M market cap, enterprise value is ~$190M. Backing that out of the same model, the price implies roughly **$34M of steady-state free cash flow** — about 13x what the base case generates. The market is not pricing the base case. It's pricing a bull case.

**Bull case** — revenue compounding 15%→10%, EBITDA margins reaching 18%, capex held to 9% of revenue, debt refinanced on better terms:

- Implied enterprise value: **~$303M**
- Implied equity: ~$174M → **~$5.00/share** on ~35M shares

**Cross-check:** $190M EV on ~$369M FY2026E revenue is 0.51x sales — optically cheap against apparel retail at 0.8–1.5x. But the business has never produced a dollar of free cash flow (FY2025: −$46M), so sales multiples flatter it. Cash flow is the binding constraint, not revenue.

## Final thoughts

The original thesis survives contact with the filings — but inverted. Yes: revenue is growing, debt was halved, losses are shrinking, and the stock is at all-time lows. But the debt wasn't cleared; it was converted into 86% of the company, and the lenders are still the marginal buyer of every new share issued. The base-case arithmetic says the equity is worth ~$0. The current $1.72 price implies a steady-state cash machine ~13x better than the base case. The bull case gets you to ~$5 — if everything goes right, including a refinancing the company hasn't earned yet.

That's not a call to action. It's what the numbers say under stated assumptions — and the assumptions, as always, are where the money is made or lost.
