On Sunday, November 8, 2026, the board of Nvidia — the world's most valuable chipmaker, worth about $4.5 trillion as of November 6 — did something boards almost never do in public: it announced an independent investigation into its own founder and chief executive. The same day, Jensen Huang sent his employees a message admitting he had been unfaithful to his wife. By Monday morning, the president of the United States, three House committee chairmen, and the Chinese foreign ministry had all spoken about the same story.

The story mixes two very different kinds of material: a set of public, on-record statements that anyone can read, and a set of espionage allegations that rest on newspaper reporting and unnamed officials. This post keeps the two apart. What each party said is quoted. What is only alleged is labeled, with the allegation's source named.

## The timeline

- **Late October 2026.** The New York Times reports that U.S. authorities investigated Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen and former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed, and concluded she was a Chinese intelligence asset. Megaspeed, a cloud computing company and Nvidia customer, had already been under U.S. scrutiny over suspicions it helped Beijing obtain advanced Nvidia chips barred from shipment to China.
- **November 4.** Three Republican House committee chairmen — Brian Mast (Foreign Affairs), John Moolenaar (Select Committee on China), and Rick Crawford (Intelligence) — write to Huang asking him to appear at a joint closed-door briefing on November 18.
- **November 6.** The Financial Times reports that U.S. officials believed Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang.
- **November 8.** Nvidia's board announces its independent investigation. Huang's message to employees is published the same day. President Trump answers reporters' questions.
- **November 9.** China's Foreign Ministry responds. Nvidia shares fall roughly 5% in Monday trading.

## What is established

These points come from the parties themselves, on the record:

1. Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation, overseen by a committee of independent directors assisted by outside independent counsel. ([Board statement, NVIDIA Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors))
2. The board says it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised," and that its inquiry "will examine all available information."
3. Huang admitted, in writing, to being unfaithful to his wife, and apologized. His message names no other person.
4. Huang supports the investigation and has committed to cooperating with it. He remains CEO, and the company says he will lead "without interruption."
5. Three House chairmen have requested a closed-door briefing on November 18. Huang has until November 11 to accept or propose another date.
6. No criminal charges have been announced against anyone in this matter, and no authority has publicly presented evidence that Nvidia information reached Beijing.

## What is alleged, and by whom

- That Alice Huang gathered information for Beijing: alleged by U.S. officials, as reported by The New York Times. Reuters has stated it could not independently verify the claims in the Times report, nor Alice Huang's current employment status with Megaspeed.
- That the relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang was romantic: reported by the Financial Times, citing officials' beliefs. Huang himself, asked in late October, told Bloomberg News his interactions with her were related to Megaspeed and were "completely normal and not unusual."
- That President Trump was briefed on the intelligence findings roughly a year ago and chose not to act: reported by The New York Times. Trump, asked about it directly, answered the question about his own conduct rather than disputing the briefing.

None of these allegations has been tested in any public proceeding. Alice Huang has made no public statement. Megaspeed did not respond to a Reuters request for comment.

## Jensen Huang's statements

Huang has spoken three times in this story: to Bloomberg News in late October (above), in his November 8 message to employees, and through the company's confirmation that he supports the board's review. The employee message, titled "A message to our company" and published on Nvidia's newsroom, is the fullest statement. Its key passages:

> "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry."

> "I take full responsibility for what I did and the hurt I caused, and I accept the criticism that comes with it."

> "NVIDIA's Board of Directors has initiated an independent investigation. I support the investigation and will cooperate fully."

> "I am committed to protecting NVIDIA and supporting America's national security."

> "My responsibility is to serve you and be worthy of your trust. I have fallen short of that responsibility. I am fully committed to NVIDIA and will work to earn back your confidence."

## The other statements

**The board** closed its statement with: "The Board has confidence in Jensen and his leadership team and will provide further updates as appropriate." It named neither the directors on its committee nor the law firm assisting them, and set no timetable.

**President Trump**, on Sunday: "I have not spoken to him about it. I just know he's a fine person, but you never know what is going on. I hope everything's fine. He's done such an incredible job." Asked whether he should have acted when briefed, he said: "No, I don't think so. I think he's an honorable guy. I think he probably has an excuse for it. You have to hear it." Secretary of State Marco Rubio declined to comment.

**The House chairmen** wrote that "recent reports raise significant national security concerns regarding the PRC's access to sensitive US technology and information" and that "it is vital that Congress determines the extent to which US national security has been placed at risk." The requested briefing covers two subjects: Nvidia's security protocols against foreign espionage, and Huang's interactions with Alice Huang.

**Beijing**, through Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun on Monday, said the allegation that Alice Huang was gathering information for China was "purely groundless speculation and an attempt to smear and malign China."

**Analysts** have focused on structure rather than evidence. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon wrote that the news "brings the stewardship of one of America's most important companies and the architect of the US AI trajectory under debate." Reuters notes Nvidia has disclosed no succession plan, and that Huang, 63, has run the company since co-founding it in 1993 with dozens of executives reporting directly to him.

## Why the structure matters

Strip away the allegations and the governance facts alone are unusual. The subject of the investigation is the founder, the public face of the company, and — by the company's own flat structure — its central decision-maker, at a firm valued at about $4.5 trillion and sitting at the center of U.S. export-control policy toward China. The board's chosen instrument is the standard one for exactly this situation: independent directors, outside counsel, no pre-judgment either way. Its statement simultaneously opens an inquiry and records that it currently sees no evidence of compromise. Both halves of that sentence are the story.

## What happens next

Three dated events will move this story, in order: **November 11**, Huang's deadline to answer the House chairmen; **November 17**, Nvidia's quarterly results after the close, where Huang is expected to face analysts for the first time since the announcement; and **November 18**, the closed-door briefing the chairmen have requested. The board's investigation has no public timetable. Until one of those produces new on-record material, the factual record is the one set out above — statements, allegations, and a calendar.
