## 1. The Result

James Talarico, a state representative and former public-school teacher, won about **55%** of the vote to Attorney General Ken Paxton's roughly **43%**, and Paxton conceded after the AP called the race (Houston Public Media). It was the most expensive Senate race in U.S. history, and the first Democratic Senate win in Texas since Lloyd Bentsen in **1988**.

The result also broke the longest streak in Texas politics: Republicans had won every statewide race since **1994**.

## 2. The Border Swing

The clearest change was along the border.

| Measure | 2024 | 2026 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cameron County margin | Trump **+6** | Talarico **+38** (early, unofficial) |
| Democratic edge in Latino party identification (NBC exit poll) | **+10** | **+16** |

_Sources: Latin Times, citing NBC News exit polling and early returns._

In 2024, Republican gains in the Rio Grande Valley were one of the defining stories of the presidential election. In 2026 they reversed. A 44-point swing in one county is large even by midterm standards; it will shrink or grow as the official count is finished, which is why it is labeled early.

The House map moved too. The seat that gave Democrats their House majority overnight was **Texas's 35th District**, redrawn in the 2025 mid-decade redistricting to favor Republicans, and won by Democrat Johnny Garcia.

## 3. What Voters Were Voting On

- **Prices.** Reporting on the border counties tied the swing to Hispanic voters' anger over high prices. Nationally, about nine in ten voters called the cost of living a crucial or major factor (CNN exit poll).
- **Immigration enforcement.** The same reporting cited the administration's enforcement campaign as a second driver.
- **The campaigns.** Talarico centered economic concerns and public education and campaigned directly for Latino voters in South Texas.

## 4. The Governor's Race and Data Centers

State Rep. Gina Hinojosa defeated three-term Gov. Greg Abbott, leading **53% to 46%** with 83% of the vote counted. She becomes the first Latina elected governor of Texas.

One part of her platform matters well beyond the state: she campaigned on a **temporary ban on new data-center construction**. Texas is one of the busiest data-center markets in the country, and on election night technology futures sold off as investors weighed a Democratic wave on AI regulation, before recovering by dawn (see our [election-night markets report](/blog/democrats-win-congress-2026-midterms)). How a governor-elect turns a moratorium pledge into policy, alongside a Republican legislature, is the Texas story to watch in 2027.

## 5. What It Means From Here

- **For the Senate:** Texas's flip was one of seven that gave Democrats control from January 3, 2027.
- **For Texas:** a Democratic governor and a Republican legislature mean negotiation on everything from the budget to data centers.
- **For 2028:** the size of the Latino swing is the number both parties will study, and the certified county results will show how much of it holds.
