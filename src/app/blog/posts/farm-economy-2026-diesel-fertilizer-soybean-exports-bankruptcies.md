## 1. Selling: The Soybean Hole

China was the largest buyer of U.S. soybeans before the trade war, and its purchases collapsed during it.

- **2025–26 exports: 1.58 billion bushels**, the lowest in 13 years (USDA).
- **The truce.** After the October 2025 U.S.-China truce, China committed to buy **25 million metric tons** of U.S. soybeans a year through 2028. Early buying disappointed: about **2.7 million tons** in the first stretch, against a **12 million ton** target officials had announced (Trading Economics).
- **The outlook.** USDA projects **1.7 billion bushels** for 2026–27, a partial recovery. Farm Progress put it plainly: China could reach the 25 MMT threshold by late 2026 or early 2027 *if* it keeps its recent pace, "and that's a big 'if.'"

## 2. Spending: Diesel and Fertilizer

The war with Iran closed or disrupted the Strait of Hormuz, through which about **a third of global fertilizer shipments** normally pass.

| Input | Before | After | Change |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Diesel (national average) | $3.76 | $5.51 | **+47%** |
| Urea | — | — | **about +50%** |
| Ammonia | — | — | **about +20%** |

_Diesel: AAA, via Axios. Fertilizer: Oxford Economics, via Fortune and Axios. Diesel change: ($5.51 − $3.76) ÷ $3.76 = 46.5%._

The American Farm Bureau Federation's survey of **5,700 farmers** found about **70%** could not afford all the fertilizer they needed, and nearly **six in ten** said their finances had worsened. Fertilizer prices eased after a tentative peace deal in June, but researchers at North Dakota State University project them to stay above pre-war levels until 2028.

## 3. The Balance Sheet: Aid and Bankruptcies

- **Aid.** USDA's Farmer Bridge Assistance program paid **$11 billion** to eligible row-crop producers and **$1 billion** to specialty-crop producers (University of Kentucky agricultural economists).
- **The downturn.** The Kansas City Fed's survey describes the longest sustained decline in farm conditions since the stretch that began in 2013 and ran to 2020–21.
- **Bankruptcies.** Chapter 12 farm bankruptcies reached **315** in 2025 (AFBF) and were up **19%** for the 12 months to June 2026 (NPR). The number is small next to roughly two million U.S. farms, but it is the leading edge of financial stress.

Aid of this kind keeps operations solvent through a bad year. It does not replace a lost export market or lower the cost of next spring's fertilizer.

The main dispute is **SNAP**: state cost-sharing and error-rate rules, and whether to restore SNAP funding cut in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Analysts expect the December spending bill to carry a **one-year farm bill extension**, possibly with disaster aid, leaving the full bill to the new Congress.

## 5. What Would Help Most

Reading the numbers rather than the platforms, three levers matter most:

1. **Export demand.** A durable China purchase schedule, or new markets, does more for soybean revenue than any aid program.
2. **Input costs.** Fertilizer and diesel are global prices; the tools here are energy policy, trade rules on imported fertilizer (most U.S. potash comes from Canada) and the Strait of Hormuz.
3. **Certainty.** A five-year farm bill instead of one-year extensions lets lenders and farmers plan.

How the new Congress approaches each is the subject of [The Democratic Congress: Priorities and the Farm Gap](/blog/democratic-congress-2027-agenda-farmers-tariffs-farm-bill).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why are U.S. farmers struggling in 2026?**
Lower export sales and higher costs at the same time: soybean exports hit a 13-year low in 2025–26, while diesel rose from $3.76 to $5.51 a gallon and urea fertilizer about 50% after the Iran war began.

**How much did diesel prices rise in 2026?**
The national average went from $3.76 to $5.51 a gallon after the war began, according to AAA, an increase of about 47%.

**Are farm bankruptcies rising?**
Yes. Chapter 12 farm bankruptcies were up 19% for the 12 months to June 2026, after 315 cases in 2025.

**Did the farm bill expire?**
The 2018 farm bill's extension expired on September 30, 2026. A stopgap keeps most programs funded through December 11, and a one-year extension is expected in the December spending bill.

**Is China buying U.S. soybeans again?**
Yes, under an October 2025 truce that commits China to 25 million metric tons a year through 2028, but early buying fell short of the targets announced.

## Conclusion and Key Takeaways

1. **Revenue fell and costs rose at once**, the worst combination for a farm balance sheet.
2. **$12 billion in aid bridged a year**, not the downturn.
3. **The farm bill is past its deadline**, with a one-year extension the likely next step.
4. **Exports, input costs and certainty** are the measures to watch for whether policy reaches the farm.
