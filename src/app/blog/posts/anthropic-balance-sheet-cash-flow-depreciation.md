## 2. Why the Net Loss Is $80 Billion

**Most of the loss is the company’s rising valuation showing up as an accounting charge.**

Anthropic has issued convertible notes that will become shares. As its valuation rose, those notes became more valuable, and under the accounting rules the increase counts as a loss.

| :--- | ---: |
| Operating loss | (693) |
| Interest income | 1,111 |
| Change in fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs | **(72,388)** |
| Other income (expense), mostly fair-value recognition of other instruments | (8,757) |
| **Net loss** | **(80,748)** |

## 5. What Changes Next

**The cost of compute is moving onto the balance sheet.** About **$161 billion** of five-year Broadcom equipment leases are accounted for as **finance leases**. As that hardware comes into service, it will show up as assets that are amortized, plus lease interest, rather than as a service expense. Spread over five years, that is on the order of **$30 billion a year** once fully in service. This is our illustration, not a company estimate.

The filing is also explicit that cash flow will turn more negative as it builds. It expects “significant net cash used in operating and investing activities for the foreseeable future,” with payments made “well in advance” of compute coming online.

**The covenant to watch:** Anthropic’s $15 billion credit line requires at least **$5 billion of liquidity** at the end of each quarter, starting December 31, 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much cash does Anthropic have?**
$112.4 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $19.3 billion of short-term investments as of June 30, 2026: $131.7 billion in total.

**Why did Anthropic lose $80 billion in the first half of 2026?**
Almost all of it is non-cash. $72.4 billion came from increases in the fair value of convertible notes and similar instruments as Anthropic’s valuation rose, and $8.8 billion from recognizing other financial instruments. Operating loss was only $693 million, and the second quarter was profitable.

**Does Anthropic generate free cash flow?**
For the first time, yes. Operating cash flow was $3.93 billion in the first half of 2026 and capital expenditure $482 million, implying about $3.45 billion of free cash flow by our calculation. The company expects heavy investment to turn cash flow negative again.
