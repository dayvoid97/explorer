## 1. The Power, Measured

From January 3, 2027:

| Fully in Democratic hands | Shared with the president | Out of reach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Committee chairs, hearings, subpoenas | Appropriations and shutdown deadlines | Any law over a veto (290 House / 67 Senate votes) |
| Senate confirmations of judges, Cabinet officers and regulators | The debt limit | The Federal Reserve's structure |
| What gets a floor vote in both chambers | The farm bill and trade legislation | |

Hakeem Jeffries is in line to become the first Black Speaker of the House, and Chuck Schumer to return as Senate majority leader (The Hill, amNY). The Tax Foundation's assessment of the tax agenda applies more broadly: the room to maneuver in 2027 is limited by the White House.

## 2. What Democrats Say They Will Do First

From House Democratic leaders' public statements and reporting on their plans:

1. **Health care.** Restore the expired Affordable Care Act premium subsidies and reverse nearly **$1 trillion** in Medicaid cuts from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Health care is the closest thing to a consensus first item (NBC News, PBS).
2. **Affordability.** A 100-day agenda organized in five working groups: **health care, housing, groceries, gas and utilities, and caregiving** (PBS).
3. **Tariffs.** Quick consideration of legislation to end the global tariffs and restore Congress's power over trade (Tax Foundation).
4. **Taxes.** Child Tax Credit and Earned Income Tax Credit expansions, alongside higher taxes on high earners (Tax Foundation).
5. **Oversight.** Investigations, with corruption allegations named as a focus (ABC News).

On paper the match is close. The difference between paper and delivery is the veto: every item in the right-hand column that requires a new law needs the president's signature, or a deal.

## 4. The Farm Gap

The farm-state voters who helped flip Iowa and Kansas face a specific list of problems (see [The 2026 Farm Economy in Numbers](/blog/farm-economy-2026-diesel-fertilizer-soybean-exports-bankruptcies)). Here is how each maps to what Congress can do.

| Farm problem | What Congress controls | The catch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Farm bill expired** (Sept. 30) | Writing and passing a five-year bill | The SNAP dispute; the president must sign |
| **Tariffs and retaliation** (soybeans, wheat, Canadian inputs) | Trade legislation, such as the Trade Review Act backed by Kansas Senator-elect Adam Hamilton (new tariffs end unless Congress approves them within 60 days) | Veto; a deal is likelier than repeal |
| **Diesel and fertilizer costs** | Hearings, appropriations, energy policy | Global prices; limited direct control |
| **Rural health care** | ACA subsidies; Medicaid funding | The same veto math |
| **Cash-flow stress** | Disaster and bridge aid in spending bills | Aid is a bridge, not a market |

**The responsibility, stated plainly.** Voters in Kansas elected a senator who promised to end the tariffs. Voters in Iowa elected one who promised to fix health care. Maine elected one who said a narrow waiver was not enough. The majority's own measure of success, in farm country, is whether those promises turn into a farm bill, a trade arrangement and lower costs that farmers can see on a balance sheet by the 2028 planting season.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the Democrats' first priorities in the new Congress?**
Health care (restoring ACA subsidies and reversing Medicaid cuts), a 100-day affordability agenda across health care, housing, groceries, gas and utilities, and caregiving, and legislation to end the global tariffs.

**Can a Democratic Congress end Trump's tariffs?**
It can pass a bill, but the president can veto it, and overriding a veto takes 290 House and 67 Senate votes. Analysts consider a veto-proof majority unlikely, so a negotiated change is more likely than outright repeal.

**What happens to the farm bill?**
The 2018 farm bill's extension expired on September 30, 2026, and a stopgap funds most programs to December 11. A one-year extension is expected in December, with the full bill left to the new Congress.

**When does the new Congress start?**
January 3, 2027.

## Conclusion and Key Takeaways

1. **Democrats hold the agenda, the hearing rooms and confirmations**, not the veto.
2. **Their stated priorities match what voters said**: prices, health care and tariffs.
3. **Farm country is the clearest test**, with a concrete list: the farm bill, trade, input costs and rural health care.
4. **Delivery will need deals.** The scorecard above is how to tell whether they are being made.
