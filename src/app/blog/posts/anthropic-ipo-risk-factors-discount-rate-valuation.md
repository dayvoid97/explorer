## The 60-Second Version

- **Measurable risks:**
  - Compute commitments of $265.6B, about 8× annualized revenue.
  - 48% of revenue through cloud marketplaces.
  - Built-in dilution.
  - Revenue that depends on models less than six months old.
- **Unmeasurable risks:**
  - Staying at the frontier, and compute supply.
  - Model behavior and copyright litigation.
  - A governance structure that limits public shareholders.
- **The discount rate:** the 10-year Treasury is at **5.00%**, and an illustrative cost of equity is **11–12%**. Because nearly all value lies years ahead, a one-point change in the discount rate can move fair value by about a fifth.
- **The hurdle:** at a reported $2 trillion, today’s annualized operating income is an earnings yield of about **0.2%**.

## 2. Qualitative Risks

- **Competition at the frontier.** OpenAI, Google and well-funded large technology companies, plus open-weight models.
- **Compute supply.** A few suppliers and geographies, supply constraints and export controls.
- **Model behavior.** The filing warns of models acting in “unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways,” and of misuse by bad actors.
- **Litigation.** The _Bartz_ settlement of $1.5 billion was approved in July 2026. Suits are pending from music publishers, Reddit, Canadian authors and news publishers, plus consumer class actions and opt-out author suits.
- **Regulation.** The EU AI Act, state attorney-general pressure for federal rules, and export controls. The filing says Anthropic’s own support for regulation could slow its growth.
- **Safety-driven release choices.** Anthropic has delayed or limited releases, such as Mythos, and may again, at a cost to near-term revenue.
- **Governance.** Control sits with a purpose trust and the founders; public shareholders “may” see the mission prioritized over financial returns.
- **Conflicted partners.** The same companies are suppliers, investors, sales channels and competitors.
- **People and culture.** Dependence on seven co-founders and key researchers.

## 4. How the Discount Rate Hits a Long-Duration Stock

**When almost all of a company’s value is in the future, small changes in the discount rate move the price a lot.**

The perpetuity formula, value = cash flow ÷ (discount rate − growth rate), shows why. If the gap between the discount rate and long-run growth is 4 points, value is 25 times cash flow. Raise the discount rate one point and the gap is 5 points: value becomes 20 times cash flow, **20% lower**.

That is why the IPO is sensitive to rates:
- **Higher Treasury yields** lift the required return on all stocks, hitting long-duration growth stocks hardest.
- **Lower yields** do the opposite.

The Federal Reserve’s next meeting and the path of long-term yields matter for this deal as much as the next quarter’s revenue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the main risks in Anthropic’s IPO?**
The measurable risks:
- $265.6 billion of compute commitments.
- Reliance on cloud marketplaces for 48% of revenue.
- Built-in dilution from convertible notes and the Public Benefit Fund.
- Revenue that depends on frequent model releases.

The unmeasurable risks:
- Frontier competition and compute supply.
- Model misbehavior and copyright litigation.
- A governance structure that limits public shareholders’ influence.

**What discount rate should be used to value Anthropic?**
There is no single answer. With the 10-year Treasury at 5.00% on October 26, 2026, a 4.5% equity risk premium and a beta of 1.3 to 1.6, the cost of equity would be roughly 11% to 12%. Anthropic itself discounts its leases at 7.1% to 7.5%.

**What price did Anthropic’s last private round imply?**
The Series H preferred shares sold for $589.01 each, which is $58.90 per common share after the 10-for-1 conversion. That implies about $1 trillion across roughly 16.9 billion fully diluted shares.
